# Patent eligibility (35 U.S.C. 101)

## 1. The statute and the court test

- 35 U.S.C. 101: any new and useful process, machine, manufacture, or composition of matter, or improvement of one, is eligible.
- Courts add three judicial exceptions: abstract ideas, laws of nature, natural phenomena.
- Alice/Mayo two-step test (*Alice Corp. v. CLS Bank Int'l*, 573 U.S. 208 (2014); *Mayo Collaborative Servs. v. Prometheus Labs.*, 566 U.S. 66 (2012)):
  1. Is the claim "directed to" a judicial exception?
  2. If yes, do the other elements, alone and as an ordered combination, add an "inventive concept" (significantly more)?
- In court, eligibility is a question of law that can rest on facts. Courts often decide it on a motion to dismiss.

## 2. The USPTO framework (MPEP 2106)

Current MPEP: 9th Ed., Rev. 01.2024 (Nov. 2024). The Dec. 5, 2025 Desjardins notice amends parts of MPEP 2106 (see section 4). Eligibility guidance lives in MPEP 2103-2106.07, the July 2024 AI update, and the memos below.

Set the broadest reasonable interpretation (BRI) first (MPEP 2106, subsection II).

| Step | Question | MPEP |
|---|---|---|
| Step 1 | Is the claim to a process, machine, manufacture, or composition? | 2106.03 |
| Step 2A, prong one | Does the claim *recite* (set forth or describe) a judicial exception? | 2106.04, 2106.04(a)-(c) |
| Step 2A, prong two | Does the claim *as a whole* integrate the exception into a practical application? | 2106.04(d) |
| Step 2B | Do the additional elements add significantly more (inventive concept)? | 2106.05 |
| Streamlined | Eligibility self-evident; full analysis not needed | 2106.06 |
| Rejections / replies | How examiners write and evaluate 101 rejections and responses | 2106.07, 2106.07(a)-(c) |

Step 2A = Alice step one. Step 2B = Alice step two. If prong one finds no exception, the claim is eligible; stop.

### Abstract idea groupings (MPEP 2106.04(a)(2))

1. Mathematical concepts: relationships, formulas or equations, calculations.
2. Certain methods of organizing human activity: fundamental economic practices (hedging, insurance, risk), commercial or legal interactions (contracts, advertising, sales), managing personal behavior or interactions (teaching, following rules).
3. Mental processes: observations, evaluations, judgments, opinions that can practically be done in the human mind or with pen and paper.

### Prong two / Step 2B considerations

Favor eligibility:
- Improvement to computer functioning or another technology or technical field (2106.04(d)(1), 2106.05(a)).
- Particular machine (2106.05(b)).
- Particular transformation of an article (2106.05(c)).
- Particular treatment or prophylaxis for a disease (2106.04(d)(2)).
- Other meaningful limitations (2106.05(e)).

Weigh against:
- Mere instructions to "apply it" on a computer (2106.05(f)).
- Insignificant extra-solution activity, such as data gathering or output (2106.05(g)).
- Field-of-use or technological-environment limits only (2106.05(h)).
- Step 2B only: well-understood, routine, conventional activity (2106.05(d)). The examiner needs factual support for this (Berkheimer line, MPEP 2106.07(a)).

## 4. 2025-2026 USPTO actions

### Aug. 4, 2025 memo (Charles Kim, Deputy Commissioner for Patents)

"Reminders on evaluating subject matter eligibility of claims under 35 U.S.C. 101." Sent to TCs 2100, 2600, 3600. Says it announces no new practice. Key points:
- Mental process grouping "is not without limits." A limitation that cannot practically be performed in the human mind is not a mental process. "Claim limitations that encompass AI in a way that cannot be practically performed in the human mind do not fall within this grouping."
- "Recites" vs "involves." Example 39 ("training the neural network ... using the first training set") does not recite an exception. Example 47 claim 2 (names backpropagation and gradient descent) does recite a mathematical concept.
- Analyze prong two with the claim as a whole. Do not evaluate additional elements "in a vacuum."
- Improvements: the specification need not state the improvement explicitly, but it must be apparent to a skilled artisan. The claim need not recite the improvement.
- Do not oversimplify claims or expand "apply it." Footnote contrasts *Recentive* with Example 47 claim 3.
- Close calls: reject only if ineligibility is more likely than not (>50%). Uncertainty alone is not enough. Preponderance standard applies (MPEP 706).
- Compact prosecution: examine every claim under 102, 103, 112 even if 101 is rejected.

### Ex parte Desjardins (ARP, Sept. 26, 2025; precedential Nov. 4, 2025)

- Appeal No. 2024-000567; Application 16/319,040; TC 2100.
- Appeals Review Panel: Director John A. Squires (author), Acting Commissioner Valencia Martin Wallace, Vice Chief APJ Michael W. Kim.
- Posture: decision on rehearing. The Board had entered a new ground of rejection under 101 for claims 1-6 and 8-20. The ARP vacated that new ground. It left the Board's other rulings alone. The claims remain rejected under 103.
- Claim: computer-implemented method of training a machine learning model on a second task while protecting performance on a first task, using a penalty term based on parameter importance (addresses "catastrophic forgetting").
- Reasoning: claim 1 "may recite an abstract idea" (a mathematical concept) but is not directed to one. At prong two, the specification identified improvements to how the model itself operates (learning new tasks while protecting old ones, less storage, less complexity). The limitation "adjust the first values ... while protecting performance ... on the first machine learning task" reflects that improvement. Relied on *Enfish*.
- Quotes: "Examiners and panels should not evaluate claims at such a high level of generality." "[Sections] 102, 103 and 112 are the traditional and appropriate tools to limit patent protection to its proper scope. These statutory provisions should be the focus of examination." "Categorically excluding AI innovations from patent protection in the United States jeopardizes America's leadership in this critical emerging technology."
- Binding on examiners and the PTAB because precedential. Not binding on courts.

### Dec. 5, 2025 advance notice of MPEP change (Kim)

Effective on issuance. Amends MPEP Rev. 01.2024:
- Adds a Desjardins paragraph to 2106.04(d), subsection III.
- Revises 2106.04(d)(1): specification must give enough detail that the improvement is apparent; a bare, conclusory assertion of improvement is not enough; claim must include the components or steps that provide the improvement but need not recite the improvement itself.
- Revises 2106.05(a): evaluate the claim "as a whole"; do not dismiss elements as "generic computer components" without asking whether they confer a technological improvement. Adds examples xiii (improved way of training an ML model that protects prior-task knowledge) and xiv (improvements to component or system performance from adjusting ML model parameters).
- Revises 2106.05(f): adds Desjardins as a technological solution to a technological problem.

### Subject Matter Eligibility Declarations (SMEDs)

- Dec. 4, 2025: Director Squires memo to examiners, "Subject Matter Eligibility Declarations." A SMED is an optional declaration under 37 CFR 1.132 (MPEP 716, 2106.07). Anyone with knowledge of the facts may sign. It cannot add matter missing from the original disclosure. It can show facts as of filing: state of the art, how the invention improved it, how a skilled artisan reads the specification. Opinions on the legal conclusion get no weight; underlying facts may. Examiners must consider a timely, formally proper SMED on the merits. The memo calls 101 "a subject matter gatekeeper."
- Dec. 4, 2025 best-practices memo to applicants: superseded by the Apr. 30, 2026 memo "Best Practices for Submission of Rule 132 Subject Matter Eligibility Declarations (SMEDs)."
- Sept. 29, 2026: Director memo "SMEDs under 37 CFR 1.132 - Flowchart for Analysis and Best Practices" (addendum to the April 2026 memo). Best practices (not requirements):
  - File the SMED separately from 102/103/112 evidence. In Patent Center use document code "AF/D.SMED", description "Subject Matter Eligibility Declaration."
  - File early, even before first action. After prosecution closes, a SMED is timely only in limited cases (MPEP 716.01).
  - Disinterested declarants weigh more. Neutral third-party sources (e.g., trade articles) help.
  - Say which step the SMED addresses (e.g., Step 2A prong one: cannot practically be performed in the mind).
  - Interviews help.
  - Each TC has subject matter experts; the Primary Examiner makes the final call.
- 101 Quick Reference Guide (Step 2) is posted on the USPTO eligibility page.

## 5. Federal Circuit decisions drafters should know (2025-2026)

Courts are not bound by USPTO guidance or Desjardins. The Federal Circuit remains stricter than the Office for generic software and ML claims.

Ineligible:
- *Recentive Analytics, Inc. v. Fox Corp.*, 134 F.4th 1205 (Fed. Cir. Apr. 18, 2025) (precedential). Machine learning used to make TV event schedules and network maps. Holding: "patents that do no more than claim the application of generic machine learning to new data environments, without disclosing improvements to the machine learning models to be applied, are patent ineligible under § 101." Iterative training and dynamic adjustment are incident to ML. The court said ML "may lead to patent-eligible improvements in technology." Cert. denied Dec. 8, 2025 (No. 25-505).
- *USAA v. PNC Bank N.A.*, 139 F.4th 1332 (Fed. Cir. 2025). Mobile check deposit claims ineligible; cited in GoTV for the rule that narrowing an abstract idea to a field does not make it concrete. (Exact decision date and later cert. denial reported in secondary sources: unconfirmed.)
- *GoTV Streaming, LLC v. Netflix, Inc.*, Nos. 24-1669, -1744 (Fed. Cir. Feb. 9, 2026) (precedential; Taranto, J.). Reversed on Netflix's cross-appeal: claims about tailoring content for wireless devices are ineligible. Claims that state functional results without saying how to achieve them in a non-abstract way fail.
- *Constellation Designs, LLC v. LG Electronics Inc.*, No. 24-1822 (Fed. Cir. Apr. 28, 2026) (precedential). Split result. "Optimization" claims reciting a constellation optimized for capacity, without how, are "abstract, result-oriented" and ineligible (relying on *O'Reilly v. Morse*). "Constellation" claims reciting specific structure (e.g., unequally spaced points, overlapping point locations) are not directed to an abstract idea and are eligible. (A modified opinion dated Aug. 31, 2026 is reported: unconfirmed.)
- *Dental Monitoring SAS v. Align Technology, Inc.*, No. 24-2270 (Fed. Cir. July 7, 2026) (nonprecedential). Deep-learning dental image claims ineligible: collect, analyze, display with a generic, known neural network; training on dental images did not make it non-generic.
- *AGI SureTrack v. Farmers Edge* (Fed. Cir. June 2026): agricultural data-collection patents ineligible, per secondary sources (unconfirmed; not read).

Eligible:
- *US Synthetic Corp. v. ITC*, No. 23-1217 (Fed. Cir. Feb. 13, 2025) (precedential; Chen, J.). Composition claims to a polycrystalline diamond compact, defined partly by magnetic properties, are not directed to an abstract idea. Reversed the ITC.
- *PowerBlock Holdings, Inc. v. iFit, Inc.*, No. 24-1177 (Fed. Cir. Aug. 11, 2025) (precedential). Motorized selectorized dumbbell claim is not directed to an abstract idea; it gives a "specific manner of performing" automated weight stacking.
- *REGENXBIO Inc. v. Sarepta Therapeutics, Inc.*, No. 24-1408 (Fed. Cir. Feb. 20, 2026) (precedential). Cultured host cells containing a recombinant nucleic acid are not directed to a natural phenomenon; analyze the claimed composition as a whole, not just whether parts occur in nature.
- *Ollnova Technologies Ltd. v. ecobee Technologies ULC*, Nos. 25-1045, -1046 (Fed. Cir. June 4, 2026) (precedential; Chen, J.). Affirmed that claims of two patents are not directed to an abstract idea: "a specific technology-based improvement in the operation of a network" (sensor polled only in an interval, transmitted only when out of range). On a third patent, vacated for erroneous eligibility jury instructions; JMOL of ineligibility denied.

Pattern across the cases: claims that recite a specific mechanism or structure survive. Claims that recite a result, a generic model, or a field of use fail.

## 6. Legislation: PERA

- Patent Eligibility Restoration Act of 2025, S. 1546 (Tillis, Coons), introduced May 1, 2025. House companion H.R. 3152 (per IPO tracker; text not read).
- Would remove all judicial exceptions and list narrow exclusions: a mathematical formula standing alone; substantially economic, financial, business, social, cultural, or artistic processes (even if a step refers to a machine); mental processes performed solely in the human mind; processes occurring in nature independent of human activity; unmodified human genes; unmodified natural materials.
- A process that cannot practically be performed without a machine or manufacture would be eligible. Adding a generic computer for data gathering or output would not confer eligibility unless the computer is necessary.
- Status: IP Subcommittee hearing Oct. 8, 2025 (secondary sources). Full Senate Judiciary Committee hearing July 14, 2026 ("From Genes to Machines: The Patent Eligibility Debate"). Listed "In Committee" as of Aug. 12, 2026. No markup or vote found as of this check (unconfirmed for Aug.-Nov. 2026). Not law.

## 7. Practical drafting checklist

All inventions:
- [ ] Write the technical problem and the technical fix in the specification in concrete detail. A bare "this improves efficiency" is not enough (MPEP 2106.04(d)(1) as amended Dec. 5, 2025).
- [ ] Make sure the independent claim contains the steps or components that produce the improvement. The claim need not say "thereby improving X."
- [ ] Claim *how*, not just the result. Avoid pure functional results (GoTV, Constellation, Recentive).
- [ ] Add dependent claims at several levels of specificity so some survive a strict court.
- [ ] Do not describe the core as "conventional," "generic," or "well known." Avoid admissions in the background.
- [ ] Keep evidence of the state of the art and measured improvements. Put favorable facts in the specification (SMED memo urges this).
- [ ] In prosecution, argue with the Aug. 4, 2025 memo, Desjardins, and MPEP 2106.05(a) examples xiii-xiv. Consider a separate SMED (code AF/D.SMED) early.

Software:
- [ ] Recite specific data structures, data flows, or architecture changes (Enfish; Desjardins; Ollnova network timing).
- [ ] Recite limitations that cannot practically be done in the mind (scale, real-time sensor or network operations).
- [ ] Tie to measurable computer-performance gains: memory, latency, bandwidth, power, storage.

AI / machine learning:
- [ ] Claim an improvement to the model or training itself (architecture, loss/penalty, parameter update rule, training pipeline), not "apply a trained model to new data" (Recentive).
- [ ] Describe why the change works and what it improves (e.g., forgetting, storage, compute).
- [ ] Balance: naming specific math (e.g., "backpropagation") makes the limitation *recite* a mathematical concept (Example 47 claim 2); generic "training the model" may only *involve* one (Example 39). Either way, put the improvement in the claim.
- [ ] Avoid "generic neural network chosen from known networks" language for the inventive part (Dental Monitoring).

Diagnostics and life sciences:
- [ ] Prefer a specific treatment step (administering a named therapy at a dose) (MPEP 2106.04(d)(2)).
- [ ] Claim engineered compositions or non-naturally occurring combinations and explain their markedly different characteristics (REGENXBIO; MPEP 2106.04(c)).
- [ ] Claim specific lab techniques or new assays as methods of preparation or measurement; pure "detect X and conclude Y" claims remain at risk in court (*Mayo*).

Business methods:
- [ ] Expect "organizing human activity." A computer doing an old business practice faster is not enough.
- [ ] Find and claim the technical layer: a new system architecture, security mechanism, or network protocol that solves a technical problem.
- [ ] If no technical improvement exists, warn the client: ineligibility risk is high in court even if the USPTO allows the claim.
