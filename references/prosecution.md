# Prosecution

## 1. Anatomy of an Office action

- Cover sheet: Office Action Summary, form PTOL-326 (MPEP 707). It shows the reply period, attachments, and the status of every claim (rejected, objected to, allowed, withdrawn).
- Non-final vs final. On second or later examination the action may be made final; the reply is then limited to appeal or amendment under 1.114 or 1.116, and objections not tied to a rejection go by petition under 1.181 (37 CFR 1.113(a)). Examiners generally make a second action final only if any new ground was necessitated by the applicant's amendment or by an IDS filed with the 1.97(c) fee (MPEP 706.07(a); standard practice, not re-read this pass).
- Reply period: usually a 3-month shortened statutory period. Extensions of time under 37 CFR 1.136(a) for fees, up to the 6-month statutory limit (35 U.S.C. 133). No reply = abandonment.
- Rejections vs objections:
  - Rejections go to patentability or claim scope: 101, 102, 103, 112(a)/(b), double patenting. Reviewed by appeal to the PTAB.
  - Objections go to form: drawings, specification informalities, claim informalities, claims "objected to as dependent on a rejected base claim but allowable if rewritten." Reviewed by petition (37 CFR 1.181), not appeal.
- Read every attachment: PTO-892 (references cited by examiner), PTO-1449/SB-08 (IDS considered), interview summary, notice of references, drawings objections.

## 2. Reply requirements (37 CFR 1.111)

- Must reply to "every ground of objection and rejection." Must "distinctly and specifically" point out supposed errors (1.111(b)).
- Must argue "the specific distinctions believed to render the claims, including any newly presented claims, patentable over any applied references." A general allegation of patentability is not enough (1.111(b)).
- If amending, "clearly point out the patentable novelty" and "show how the amendments avoid such references or objections" (1.111(c)).
- May ask to hold form objections in abeyance until allowable subject matter is found (1.111(b)).
- Supplemental replies are entered only in limited cases (1.111(a)(2)).
- The reply must be a bona fide attempt to advance prosecution. An incomplete but bona fide reply may get a notice and time to complete (MPEP 714.03).

### Reply template

1. Header: application number, filing date, examiner, art unit, confirmation number, docket number, title of paper ("Reply to Office Action of [date]").
2. Amendments to the Specification (if any).
3. Amendments to the Claims (complete claim listing; new page).
4. Amendments to the Drawings (replacement sheets labeled).
5. Remarks: status of claims; interview summary (if any); response to each objection and rejection in order; conclusion.
6. Signature of a natural person (practitioner or pro se applicant).

## 3. Amendment format (37 CFR 1.121)

- Amend a claim by rewriting the entire claim. Every amendment that touches the claims must include a complete listing of all claims ever presented, in ascending order, starting on a separate sheet (1.121(c), (c)(1)).
- Status identifier after each claim number, in parentheses: (Original), (Currently amended), (Canceled), (Withdrawn), (Previously presented), (New), (Not entered). "Withdrawn - currently amended" is allowed.
- Markings, only for "currently amended" (and amended withdrawn) claims, relative to the immediate prior version:
  - Added text: underline.
  - Deleted text: strike-through.
  - Deletion of five or fewer consecutive characters: may use double brackets, e.g., [[eroor]].
  - Use double brackets if strike-through cannot be easily perceived.
- Clean text (no markings) for Original, Withdrawn, Previously presented, and New claims. No text for Canceled or Not entered claims. Consecutive canceled claims may be grouped ("Claims 1-5 (Canceled)").
- Cancel by instruction or by "Canceled" status. A canceled claim can come back only as a New claim with a new number (1.121(c)(5)).
- Specification amendments: replacement paragraphs with markings, or a substitute specification (1.121(b), 1.125). Drawings: replacement sheets (1.121(d)).
- No new matter (35 U.S.C. 132(a)). Cite support (paragraph numbers) for every amendment in the Remarks.

## 4. Interviews (MPEP 713)

- Interviews are encouraged when they help mutual understanding (MPEP 713; 713.01). No interviews just to "sound out" the examiner (713.03). The substance must be made of record (713.04; 37 CFR 1.133(b)).
- Request with the Automated Interview Request (AIR) form. Since Mar. 9, 2026 it is in Patent Center under "Existing Submissions." Examiner responds within two business days. Questions: ExaminerInterviewPractice@uspto.gov.
- USPTO Interview Best Practices (May 2026): send an agenda in advance with any proposed amendment; keep reviewable material to about 30 minutes; expect limited examiner availability in the weeks before Dec. 31, Mar. 31, June 30, Sept. 30.
- FY2026 examiner performance plan (effective Oct. 1, 2025): reported to credit examiners for one interview hour per application or RCE, with more needing supervisor approval (secondary sources; internal document not reviewed: unconfirmed). Practical: ask for the interview after the first (non-final) action, not only after final.
- Pre-first-action interviews are possible in limited cases (MPEP 713.02).

## 5. After final (37 CFR 1.116)

- Entered as of right: cancel claims or comply with an express form requirement (1.116(b)(1)).
- May be entered: amendments putting rejected claims in better form for appeal (1.116(b)(2)), or amendments touching the merits with "good and sufficient reasons why the amendment is necessary and was not earlier presented" (1.116(b)(3)).
- Filing an after-final reply does not stop the clock (1.116(c)). File within 2 months of the final action to get an advisory action and fee-saving extension treatment (MPEP 706.07(f) - standard practice; not re-read this pass).
- AFCP 2.0 is gone: no requests accepted after Dec. 14, 2024 (89 FR 79899).
- Options after final: after-final amendment; interview; RCE; notice of appeal (with or without pre-appeal request); continuation; abandonment.

## 6. Request for continued examination (37 CFR 1.114)

- Available when prosecution is closed (final action, notice of allowance, or under appeal), before the earliest of issue-fee payment, abandonment, or a Federal Circuit appeal / civil action (1.114(a)-(b)).
- Requires a submission plus the RCE fee (1.17(e)). A submission can be an IDS, amendment, new arguments, or new evidence; if a reply is outstanding it must meet 1.111 (1.114(c)).
- Finality is withdrawn and the submission is entered (1.114(d)).
- RCEs are not available for provisional applications, design applications, international design applications, utility or plant applications filed before June 8, 1995, PCT applications not complying with 371, or patents under reexamination (1.114(e)).

## 7. Appeal

- Notice of appeal with fee once any claim has been twice rejected, within the reply period (37 CFR 41.31(a)(1); 1.134). Appeal brief within two months of the notice; the brief fee is $0, but an appeal forwarding fee is due after an examiner's answer (41.37(a); 41.20(b)(4); 41.45). Missing the brief deadline dismisses the appeal (41.37(b)). Examiner's answer (41.39). One reply brief within two months of the answer (41.41(a)); no new evidence or amendments in it (41.41(b)). Oral hearing request (41.47). Extensions of the brief period are under 1.136(a) (standard practice).
- Pre-appeal brief conference (MPEP 1204.02): file a "Pre-Appeal Brief Request for Review" with the notice of appeal and before the brief; form PTO/AIA/33; arguments may not exceed five pages; no after-final amendment may accompany it; limited to clear errors in appealable matters. Panel: at least a supervisor and the examiner. From the Sept. 2026 OG notice, a PTAB administrative patent judge may sit as a third, advisory panelist; that judge will not sit on the later PTAB panel in the case.
- Appeal conference (MPEP 1207.01): mandatory after an acceptable brief; a PTAB judge may now substitute as the third conferee (Sept. 2026 OG notice).
- Fast-Track Appeals Pilot: petition to expedite a docketed ex parte appeal; extended to May 6, 2028, with a 4-month decision target (91 FR 24524, May 6, 2026). Fee: see fees.md.
- Director-level review: the Appeals Review Panel (ARP) can rehear ex parte appeal decisions (e.g., Desjardins 2025, Baurin 2026).
- Further review: Federal Circuit (35 U.S.C. 141) or civil action in E.D. Va. (35 U.S.C. 145).

## 8. Restriction and election (35 U.S.C. 121; 37 CFR 1.142-1.146)

- 1.142: if two or more independent and distinct inventions are claimed, the examiner requires election of one. Non-elected claims are withdrawn.
- 1.143: applicant may request reconsideration (traverse) with reasons; must still elect. Without a traverse, the right to petition is lost.
- 1.144: petition to the Director after a final requirement; can be deferred until after final action on the elected claims, but not later than appeal.
- 1.145: claims added later to a different invention can be restricted out.
- 1.146: election of species when a generic claim and multiple species are claimed; if the generic claim is allowed, species claims in proper dependent form are rejoined.
- Standard for restriction: independent or distinct inventions plus a serious search or examination burden (MPEP 803).
- Safe harbor: a divisional filed because of a restriction requirement is protected from double patenting over the parent (35 U.S.C. 121, third sentence). Keep the divisional consonant with the restriction; filing a continuation instead may lose the safe harbor (general case law; not re-verified this pass).
- Reply to a restriction: elect, traverse with specific reasons if you disagree, identify claims readable on the election, and state whether any claim is generic.

## 9. Double patenting and terminal disclaimers

- Statutory double patenting: same invention claimed twice (35 U.S.C. 101). Cannot be cured by terminal disclaimer.
- Nonstatutory (obviousness-type) double patenting (OTDP): claims not patentably distinct from claims of a commonly owned patent or application. Cured by a terminal disclaimer under 37 CFR 1.321(c) (or 1.321(d) for JRA), which ties the expiration date and requires common ownership for enforceability (MPEP 804, 804.02).
- Terminal disclaimer proposal: the May 10, 2024 NPRM (89 FR 40439) would have made a TD unenforceable if any claim of the tied patent was found invalid over prior art. It was withdrawn Dec. 4, 2024 (89 FR 96152). Current TD practice is unchanged.
- Case law:
  - *In re Cellect, LLC*, 81 F.4th 1216 (Fed. Cir. 2023): OTDP is judged against the expiration date including patent term adjustment (holding stated from prior knowledge; opinion not re-read this pass).
  - *Allergan USA, Inc. v. MSN Labs.*, 111 F.4th 1358 (Fed. Cir. 2024): "a first-filed, first-issued, later-expiring claim cannot be invalidated by a later-filed, later-issued, earlier-expiring reference claim having a common priority date" (quoted in Baurin).
- *Ex parte Baurin*, Appeal 2024-002920, Application 17/135,529 (ARP Aug. 6, 2026) (precedential). Panel: Director Squires, Chief Judge Deshpande, Acting Deputy Chief Judge Ankenbrand. Reversed the Board and sustained the examiner's OTDP rejections of a 2020 continuation (priority to 2012). Held the *Allergan* exception does not apply on these facts ("first-filed" means actual filing dates). Held the Federal Circuit recognizes the anti-harassment rationale (separate owners of obvious variants) as a basis for OTDP even without term extension. Said the Office would welcome Federal Circuit clarification, now pending in *In re Ablynx N.V.*, No. 26-1333. Proposed a possible future OTDP framework if the court rejects the anti-harassment basis.
- Practice: before filing a TD, check whether the claims are actually distinct; a TD can be argued later as an admission in some contexts (general caution). Keep ownership common if a TD is filed.

## 10. Prosecution history estoppel and disclaimer

- Narrowing amendments made for patentability presumptively surrender equivalents for that element. The patentee can rebut by showing the equivalent was unforeseeable, the reason for amendment was tangential to it, or some other reason (*Festo Corp. v. Shoketsu Kinzoku Kogyo Kabushiki Co.*, 535 U.S. 722 (2002); *Warner-Jenkinson Co. v. Hilton Davis Chem. Co.*, 520 U.S. 17 (1997)).
- Prosecution disclaimer: clear and unmistakable statements in arguments can narrow claim meaning even without amendment (*Omega Eng'g, Inc. v. Raytek Corp.*, 334 F.3d 1314 (Fed. Cir. 2003)).
- Drafting rules for remarks:
  - Argue only what is needed to overcome the rejection.
  - Do not characterize "the invention" broadly or disparage alternatives.
  - Do not concede that unamended claims were anticipated or obvious.
  - Prefer adding dependent-claim limitations already examined over new, narrower language.
  - Remember amendments and arguments are public and used in litigation and IPR.

## 11. Faster examination options

- Track One prioritized examination (37 CFR 1.102(e)): fee required; no more than 4 independent claims, 30 total claims, and no multiple dependent claims; not for designs, reissues, or provisionals. Annual cap 20,000 accepted requests from FY2025 (90 FR 29990, effective July 8, 2025). Fees: see fees.md.
- Accelerated Examination: discontinued for utility petitions filed on or after July 10, 2025 (90 FR 24324). Petitions to make special for age or health remain, without fee (1.102(c)).
- Design expedited examination ("rocket docket"): suspended Apr. 17, 2025; regulations removed Aug. 14, 2025 (90 FR 39124).
- Streamlined Claim Set Pilot (90 FR 48579): petitions to make special accepted from Oct. 27, 2025 until Oct. 27, 2026 or about 200 applications per TC. Pending, unexamined utility applications with no more than 1 independent claim and 10 total claims (a preliminary amendment can comply). Special status until the first action. Status after Oct. 27, 2026: unconfirmed (likely closed unless extended).
- PCT Informed Examination Request (PIER) Pilot: selects applications from Apr. 9, 2026 to Apr. 9, 2027 (91 FR 17949) (terms not read).

## 12. Other 2024-2026 changes

- Automated Search Pilot (ASAP!) (90 FR 48161, Oct. 8, 2025): for original, noncontinuing, nonprovisional utility applications filed under 111(a) on or after Oct. 20, 2025. Petition under 37 CFR 1.182 (fee 1.17(f)). The USPTO runs an automated search and sends an Automated Search Results Notice (ASRN) before examination. No response required; applicants may file a preliminary amendment, request deferral, or expressly abandon for a fee refund. Original window closed Apr. 20, 2026. USPTO announced an extension; secondary sources report a June 1, 2026 deadline, a 3,200-application target, and fee waiver for petitions on or after Mar. 23, 2026 (unconfirmed beyond the announcement title). Status after June 2026: unconfirmed.
- Examiner AI search: from Oct. 24, 2025, examiners must use PE2E Similarity Search for utility and plant applications and record the queries and results in the search notes (MPEP 719.05(II)(C) advance notice).
- Continuing-application fee (37 CFR 1.17(w)), from the FY2025 fee rule (89 FR 91898, Nov. 20, 2024): benefit claim filed more than 6 and up to 9 years after the earliest benefit date: $2,700 / $1,080 small / $540 micro; more than 9 years: $4,000 / $1,600 / $800, less any amount already paid. See fees.md for effective date and other fees.
- Continuation limits: no USPTO Federal Register proposal to cap continuations was published from Jan. 2025 through Nov. 2026 (checked the USPTO Federal Register document list). Reports of such a proposal could not be confirmed.
- Foreign-domiciled applicants and patent owners must use a registered practitioner from July 20, 2026 (37 CFR 1.31(a)(2)-(3)). See practitioner-rules.md.
- Revival and delayed claims: petitions filed after Aug. 13, 2026 need additional information if the delay exceeds one year (was two years) (91 FR 37826).
- Eligibility: Desjardins, SMEDs, and the Dec. 5, 2025 MPEP notice change 101 replies. See eligibility-101.md.
- PTAB trial rules: proposed changes to IPR practice and discretionary denial (90 FR 48335, 90 FR 48342, Oct. 17, 2025; 90 FR 50492, Nov. 7, 2025). Not covered here.
- Ex parte reexamination: proposed rule to require third-party requesters to identify all real parties in interest (91 FR 46038, July 22, 2026) (proposal only).

## 13. Office action response checklist

- [ ] Docket the reply date, extension cost, and statutory deadline.
- [ ] List every objection and rejection, claim by claim.
- [ ] Check each reference's prior-art date and any 102(b) exception or provisional-support issue.
- [ ] Decide: argue, amend, declaration (1.130 / 1.132 / SMED), interview, or a combination.
- [ ] Draft amendments in 1.121 format with support cites.
- [ ] Answer every ground; do not leave any rejection unaddressed.
- [ ] Keep arguments narrow to limit estoppel and disclaimer.
- [ ] Consider an interview early, with an agenda.
- [ ] After final: consider 1.116 amendment, RCE, pre-appeal request (5 pages), appeal, or continuation.
- [ ] Human review and natural-person signature before filing.
