# Entity status: small and micro

| Status | Discount on most fees | Rule | How you claim it |
|---|---|---|---|
| Large (undiscounted) | 0% | default | nothing |
| Small entity | 60% | 37 CFR 1.27 | written **assertion** (any clear signed statement or checkbox) or payment of the exact small entity basic filing fee |
| Micro entity | 80% | 37 CFR 1.29 | signed **certification** on PTO/SB/15A or PTO/SB/15B (or an equivalent paper) |

Some fees have no discount: IDS size fees, disclaimer fees, certificate of correction and others. Micro entities pay the small entity amount for the paper filing fee. See fees.md.

## Small entity (37 CFR 1.27)

### Who qualifies

The applicant and **every party that holds any rights in the invention** must be one of these:

1. **Person.** An inventor or other individual who has not assigned, granted, conveyed or licensed any rights, and has no duty to do so. A person who has transferred some rights still qualifies if everyone who received rights also qualifies (1.27(a)(1)).
2. **Small business concern.** It must meet the SBA size standard: **no more than 500 employees, including affiliates** (13 CFR 121.802). It must also not have assigned, licensed or promised rights to a party that does not qualify (1.27(a)(2)).
3. **Nonprofit organization.** This covers a university or other institution of higher education located **in any country**, a 501(c)(3) tax-exempt organization, a nonprofit scientific or educational organization under a US state statute, or a foreign nonprofit that would qualify if it were in the US. It must also not have transferred rights to a party that does not qualify (1.27(a)(3)).

Traps:

- **Any license counts.** A non-exclusive license to a large entity disqualifies the applicant. So does a royalty-free license (MPEP 509.02, citing *Ulead Systems v. Lex Computer*, 351 F.3d 1139 (Fed. Cir. 2003)).
- **These do not count as licenses:** implied licenses from selling products, and an order to a firm to build a prototype for the applicant's own use (MPEP 509.02).
- **A security interest** (for example, a bank loan secured by the patent) does not count unless the borrower defaults (1.27(a)(5)).
- **Certain government use licenses** do not block small status (1.27(a)(4)). They do block micro status (1.29(a)(1)).

### How to assert it

- **Written assertion** (1.27(c)(1)). It must be clearly identifiable and signed, and it must convey entitlement. No magic words are needed. USPTO transmittal forms and the SB/16 provisional cover sheet have a checkbox for this.
- **Who may sign** (1.27(c)(2)): the applicant, a practitioner of record or acting in a representative capacity, the inventor or a joint inventor if the inventor is the applicant, or the assignee.
- **Paying the exact small entity basic filing fee** also asserts status (1.27(c)(3)). This applies to the provisional, utility and other basic filing fees. The USPTO calls this a "safety net". It encourages a written assertion as well (MPEP 509.03).
- **One assertion per application** is enough (1.27(e)).
- **Each new application needs its own assertion.** This includes continuations, divisionals, CIPs, CPAs and reissues (1.27(c)(4)).
- **E-filing bonus.** A small entity that e-files an original utility application pays $70 for the basic filing fee (fee code 4011), not $140 (1.27(b)(2); 35 U.S.C. 41(h)(3)).
- **Refund window.** If you paid a full fee and later find you were small, you may get the excess back. You must file the assertion and a refund request **within 3 months of the payment**. This period cannot be extended (1.28(a)).

### Duty to check again, and losing status

- Once established, small fees may be paid until the **issue fee or a maintenance fee** comes due. Before paying either one, you must decide again whether you still qualify (1.27(g)(1)).
- If status was lost, file a **notification of loss of entitlement** before or with the earliest issue fee or maintenance fee due after the loss. A party under 1.33(b) must sign it. Paying the large-entity amount is **not** notice (1.27(g)(2)).

### Mistakes and false claims

- **Good-faith error.** It is excused if you pay the deficiency (1.28(c)).
  - Make a separate submission for each application or patent.
  - Itemize each fee type, the small amount paid and the date, the current large amount, and each shortfall.
  - The shortfall is measured against the **current** large-entity fee on the day you pay in full.
- **Bad faith.** Claiming status "improperly, and with intent to deceive" is fraud on the Office (1.27(h)). 37 CFR 1.56(a) says no patent will be granted on an application where fraud on the Office was practiced or attempted.
- **Fine.** Since the Unleashing American Innovators Act (Dec 29, 2022), the Director may fine anyone who falsely asserts a fee reduction under 35 U.S.C. 41. The fine is **at least 3 times the amount underpaid**. It applies whether the false assertion is found before or after the patent issues (35 U.S.C. 41(j)).

## Micro entity (37 CFR 1.29)

There are two routes. Both require that the applicant **first qualify as a small entity** without relying on a government use license exception (1.29(a)(1), (d)(1)).

### Route A: gross income basis (PTO/SB/15A)

Every one of these must be true (1.29(a)):

1. **Small entity.** See above.
2. **Application limit.** Neither the applicant nor any inventor or joint inventor has been named on **more than 4 previously filed** US patent applications.
3. **Income limit.** Neither the applicant nor any inventor had gross income above the limit in the **calendar year before the year the fee is paid**.
4. **Income limit on owners.** No license or ownership interest has been given, or is owed, to an entity whose prior-year gross income was above the limit.

#### The income limit currently in force

| Fee payment date | Maximum qualifying gross income |
|---|---|
| **Sept 15, 2026 to present** | **$262,380** |
| Sept 9, 2025 to Sept 14, 2026 | $251,190 |
| Sept 10, 2024 to Sept 8, 2025 | $241,830 |
| Sept 12, 2023 to Sept 9, 2024 | $223,740 |

- The limit is 3 times the US median household income for the prior year, as reported by the Census Bureau. It usually changes in September or October each year (USPTO micro entity page).
- **Which year's income counts.** For a fee paid in 2026, each person's **2025** gross income must be at or below the limit in force on the payment date. For a fee paid in 2027, it is the **2026** income.
- **What "gross income" means** (26 U.S.C. 61(a)). For individuals it is Form 1040 "total income". For partnerships it is Form 1065 total income. For corporations it is Form 1120 total income.
- **Joint tax returns.** Each person uses what they would report filing separately.
- **Foreign income.** Convert it at the IRS yearly average exchange rate (1.29(c)).

#### Counting the prior applications

Applications that **count** toward the limit of 4:

- US nonprovisional applications of any kind (utility, design, plant, continuation, divisional).
- US reissue applications.
- US national stage (PCT section 371) applications.
- Hague international design applications that designate the US.

Age, status (pending, patented or abandoned) and whether a discount was claimed **do not matter** (USPTO micro page, section 2.2.1).

Applications that **do not count**:

- Foreign applications.
- Provisional applications.
- PCT applications for which the US basic national fee was never paid (1.29(a)(2)).
- Any application the person assigned, or had to assign, **as a result of previous employment** (1.29(b)). The duty must come from a former job, not from the person's own business (MPEP 509.04(a)).

Other counting rules:

- **Each person counts separately.** Each inventor, and each individual (non-corporate) applicant, must meet the limit on their own.
- **The limit is permanent.** Once a person passes it, **no later application** naming that person can be micro on the income basis. That includes a provisional. The USPTO example: five design applications filed, then a provisional, means no micro for the provisional.

### Route B: institution of higher education basis (PTO/SB/15B)

The applicant must be a small entity (1.29(d)). **And** one of these must be true:

- The applicant's employer, which pays the **majority of the applicant's income**, is a US institution of higher education as defined in 20 U.S.C. 1001(a).
- The applicant has assigned, or is obligated to assign, a license or other ownership interest in **this application** to such an institution.

Limits (USPTO micro page, section 3):

- The **university itself cannot be the micro entity applicant.**
- The institution must be **in a US state.** That includes DC, Puerto Rico, Guam, American Samoa, the US Virgin Islands, the Northern Mariana Islands, and the Freely Associated States (Marshall Islands, Micronesia, Palau). Foreign universities do not qualify.
- These **do not qualify**: nonprofit research foundations, technology transfer organizations, federal labs, for-profit schools, and graduate-only institutions.

### How to certify

- **Which form.** File PTO/SB/15A (income basis) or PTO/SB/15B (higher-ed basis). The current revision of both is **11-26**. Both are fillable AcroForms (see forms-and-filing.md).
- **Who signs.** A party under 37 CFR 1.33(b) signs (1.29(e)).
  - When joint inventors are together the applicant, **each joint inventor signs a separate certification**. The SB/15A has a checkbox stating that the other inventors' forms are attached.
  - A pro se applicant cannot sign for co-inventors.
- **Timing.** File it **before or with** the first fee paid at the micro rate (1.29(f)). For a new application, put the first named inventor and title at the top of the form.
- **One certification per application.** It stays in effect until changed (1.29(g)). Each continuing or reissue application needs its own (1.29(e)).
- **Check again before every payment.** You may pay a micro fee only if status is right **on the day you pay** (1.29(g)). You do not re-file the form each time. But you must check income and the application count again before each fee, especially across a new calendar year or after a September limit change.
- **Foreign domicile, effective July 20, 2026.** If any applicant is domiciled outside the US and its territories, a registered practitioner must sign the micro certification. This applies to all papers received on or after July 20, 2026, whatever the filing date (USPTO micro page; 37 CFR 1.31(a)(2)).
- **Juristic applicants.** A company applicant must always act through a registered practitioner (1.31(a)(1)).

### Losing micro status

- File a notification of loss **before or with the next fee** paid after status ends (1.29(i)). Form **PTO/SB/460** may be used. A 1.33(b) party signs it. Paying a larger amount is not notice.
- Losing micro status does **not** remove small status unless the notice says so (1.29(i)). Pay the small amount if you are still small.

### Mistakes and false certifications

- **Good-faith error.** It is excused by a separate, itemized deficiency payment for each application (1.29(k)).
  - The shortfall is measured against the **current** small or large fee on the day you pay in full.
  - If the paper is defective, the USPTO may charge a processing fee. Or it may give **1 month, not extendable**, to fix it (1.29(k)(3)).
  - Paying the deficiency counts as notice of loss (1.29(k)(4)).
- **Fraud.** Intentional false certification is fraud on the Office (1.29(j)).
- **Fine.** A false certification carries a fine of **at least 3 times the amount underpaid** (35 U.S.C. 123(f)). It can be avoided only by showing good faith after a reasonable inquiry (warning on SB/15A, citing 37 CFR 11.18(b)(2)).

## Decision steps for the skill

1. List everyone who has or will have rights: inventors, applicants, assignees, licensees, and anyone owed an assignment or license.
2. Does any of them fail the small test? Examples: a company with more than 500 employees including affiliates, or **any** license or promise to one. If so, the status is **large**.
3. Else, check the higher-ed basis (main employer is a US college or university, or rights go to one). If it fits, the status is **micro via SB/15B**.
4. Else, check the income basis. For each inventor and each individual applicant:
   - more than 4 counted prior applications means not micro;
   - prior-year gross income above $262,380 (for fees paid from Sept 15, 2026) means not micro;
   - any owner or licensee above $262,380 means not micro.
   If all pass, the status is **micro via SB/15A**.
5. Otherwise the status is **small** (written assertion).
6. Store the date of the check. Before **every** later micro fee, and before the issue fee and each maintenance fee for small status, check again. Re-check whenever the calendar year rolls over or the September income limit changes.
7. If any applicant is domiciled outside the US, stop. A registered practitioner must sign from July 20, 2026.
