# PTAB trials — case law and edge cases

**Scope.** AIA trials at the Patent Trial and Appeal Board: inter partes review (IPR), post-grant review (PGR), the expired covered-business-method program (CBM), and derivation. Ex parte reexamination, reissue, and supplemental examination are in [`prosecution.md`](prosecution.md) section 7; office-action and appeal procedure in [`../prosecution.md`](../prosecution.md); claim construction doctrine in [`claims-and-construction.md`](claims-and-construction.md).

**How to use it.** Phase 2 invalidity mode, FTO next steps, and portfolio risk load this file. Before recommending a PTAB challenge or assessing one against a client's patent, run the edge-case playbook and the checklist at the end, and re-check section 8 (discretionary denial), which changed four times between Feb. 2025 and Nov. 2026.

**Marks.** `CB p.` is the casebook PDF page.

**Casebook cautions.** (1) The casebook says the Supreme Court approved BRI in *SAS*; it was *Cuozzo* (CB p. 587). (2) It says a derivation petition is due within one year after the disputed claim issues; the statute runs one year from the earlier of the grant of the patent or the publication of the earlier application containing the claim (35 U.S.C. 135(a)(2)). (3) The casebook predates the 2018 Phillips rule, the 2020 and 2024 amendment rules, Arthrex, and the 2025–2026 institution overhaul.

## 2. Constitutionality and structure

### Oil States Energy Servs., LLC v. Greene's Energy Grp., LLC, 584 U.S. 325, 138 S. Ct. 1365 (2018) · CB p. 591
- **Facts:** Oil States sued Greene's Energy on a wellhead-protection patent; Greene's petitioned for IPR. The district court construed the claims in a way that defeated Greene's prior-art theory, but the Board, using its own analysis, held the claims anticipated. Oil States argued that only an Article III court with a jury may cancel an issued patent.
- **Holding / rule:** IPR does not violate Article III or the Seventh Amendment. Granting a patent is a public-rights matter, and IPR is simply "reconsideration of the Government's decision to grant a public franchise"; patents issue subject to that reserved authority. Once Congress properly assigns a matter to a non-Article III tribunal, the Seventh Amendment does not require a jury.
- **Nuance:** Expressly narrow: the Court did not decide retroactivity, due process, or takings challenges, whether infringement could be tried outside Article III, or whether IPR would survive without any court review (Federal Circuit review exists under 319). Justice Gorsuch, joined by the Chief Justice, dissented: by the founding, English patents could be revoked only by courts, and patents were treated as private property. The opinion's "public franchise" framing is what later Directors invoke to justify summary institution decisions (Nov. 10, 2026 memo, citing Oil States).
- **Status:** Good law as of 2026-11-18. Structure modified by *Arthrex* (below). Retroactive application of IPR to pre-AIA patents is not a taking: *Celgene Corp. v. Peter*, 931 F.3d 1342 (Fed. Cir. 2019). The Board has jurisdiction over IPRs of expired patents: *Apple Inc. v. Gesture Tech. Partners, LLC*, 127 F.4th 364 (Fed. Cir. 2025), cert. denied, No. 24-1280 (U.S. Nov. 17, 2025).
- **Skill rule:** FTO / invalidity mode: never describe an issued patent as safe from administrative cancellation because it is old, expired, or litigated; IPR (when instituted) and reexamination remain available, and a court's favorable construction does not bind the Board.

### United States v. Arthrex, Inc., 594 U.S. 1 (2021) · supplemental (not in casebook)
- **Facts:** Smith & Nephew petitioned for IPR of an Arthrex patent; three APJs held the claims unpatentable. Arthrex argued that APJs, appointed by the Secretary of Commerce, were acting as principal officers because no Senate-confirmed officer could review their final decisions; 35 U.S.C. 6(c) said only the Board could grant rehearing. The Federal Circuit had "fixed" the problem by stripping APJs' removal protections.
- **Holding / rule:** Parts I–II (five Justices): the APJs' unreviewable power to issue final decisions binding the Executive Branch is incompatible with appointment as inferior officers; an inferior officer's decisions must be reviewable by a principal officer. Part III (plurality of four, with three more Justices joining the disposition): the remedy is to make 6(c) unenforceable only to the extent it blocks Director review. The Director "may review final PTAB decisions and, upon review, may issue decisions himself on behalf of the Board." Arthrex got a remand to the Acting Director to decide whether to rehear, not a new APJ panel, because the APJs were validly appointed to decide in the first instance.
- **Nuance:** The holding is limited to IPR adjudications; the Court reserved the Director's supervision of other Board work (for example, ex parte appeals). Justice Gorsuch agreed there was a violation but would have set the decision aside rather than rewrite the statute. Justice Breyer (with Sotomayor and Kagan) would have upheld the statute but joined the remedy. Justice Thomas (joined in part by the three) dissented: APJs are inferior officers already supervised by the Director. The Court said indirect controls (stacking panels, de-designating judges) "blur the lines of accountability," which matters for any future structural challenge.
- **Status:** Good law as of 2026-11-18. Implemented first by interim Director Review procedures and then by final rules, "Rules Governing Director Review of PTAB Decisions," 89 FR 79744 (Oct. 1, 2024, eff. Oct. 31, 2024). On remand, review by the Commissioner for Patents performing the Director's functions was upheld: *Arthrex, Inc. v. Smith & Nephew, Inc.*, 35 F.4th 1328 (Fed. Cir. 2022). Arthrex's accountability theme is the stated basis for the Director's 2025 takeover of institution decisions (section 8).
- **Skill rule:** Invalidity mode / portfolio: after any final written decision, list Director Review as a step before appeal (check 37 CFR 42.75 and the current Director Review process page for the deadline and format), and record who decided each stage (Director vs. panel), because that affects reviewability.

## 3. Institution and judicial review

### Cuozzo Speed Techs., LLC v. Lee, 579 U.S. 261, 136 S. Ct. 2131 (2016) · CB p. 608
- **Facts:** Garmin petitioned for IPR of Cuozzo's GPS speed-limit speedometer patent. The Board instituted on claim 17 and also on claims 10 and 14, from which claim 17 depends, reasoning that Garmin had implicitly challenged them on the same art; it then canceled all three under the broadest-reasonable-construction rule and denied a motion to amend that it found enlarged the claims.
- **Holding / rule:** (1) 35 U.S.C. 314(d) ("final and nonappealable") bars judicial review of a challenge that is essentially an attack on the decision to institute under the institution statutes, such as a claim that the petition lacked particularity or that the Board instituted on grounds not expressly pleaded for a claim. (The casebook excerpt omits this part; the SAS excerpt at CB p. 640 restates it.) (2) The USPTO's regulation adopting broadest reasonable construction in IPR was a reasonable exercise of its 316(a)(4) rulemaking power: BRI encourages precise claims, the Office had used it for a century, and inconsistency with courts is built into a two-track system with different burdens.
- **Nuance:** The Court left room to review constitutional questions and agency action outside statutory limits ("shenanigans"). On amendments, it pointed to the one motion to amend in 316(d) as making BRI fair, despite low grant rates. The BRI holding rested on Chevron-style deference, which *Loper Bright Enters. v. Raimondo*, 603 U.S. 369 (2024) later overruled; express procedural rulemaking delegations (316(a)) are unaffected.
- **Status:** The 314(d) holding is good law and has been extended (*Thryv*, below). The BRI holding is superseded for AIA trials by rule: for petitions filed on or after Nov. 13, 2018, the Board construes claims as a court would under 35 U.S.C. 282(b), i.e., Phillips, and considers prior court or ITC constructions made of record (83 FR 51340; 37 CFR 42.100(b)). BRI still governs examination, reissue, and ex parte reexamination.
- **Skill rule:** Invalidity mode: treat any argument that the Board should not have instituted as unappealable; build every challenge to win on the final written decision, not on institution error.

### SAS Institute, Inc. v. Iancu, 584 U.S. 357, 138 S. Ct. 1348 (2018) · CB p. 633
- **Facts:** SAS challenged all 16 claims of ComplementSoft's patent. Under its "partial institution" rule (37 CFR 42.108(a)), the Board instituted on only some claims and its final decision ignored the rest.
- **Holding / rule:** 35 U.S.C. 318(a) requires a final written decision on "any patent claim challenged by the petitioner," and here "any" means "every." The Director's choice under 314(b) is binary: institute the review the petition asks for, or don't. The petitioner, as master of its petition, defines the proceeding. Section 314(d) did not bar review because SAS challenged the scope of the review, an issue of statutory authority, not the decision to institute.
- **Nuance:** Dissents (Ginsburg; Breyer, joined by three) would have let the agency curate claims for efficiency. The decision did not limit the Director's discretion over whether to institute at all, which is the door later policies use. The Federal Circuit applied SAS to grounds as well as claims (*PGS Geophysical AS v. Iancu*, 891 F.3d 1354 (Fed. Cir. 2018)).
- **Status:** Good law as of 2026-11-18; codified at 85 FR 79120 (Dec. 9, 2020: institution on all challenged claims and all grounds). The Nov. 10, 2026 memo invokes SAS's all-or-nothing rule to justify denying institution when a petition fails for a "substantial majority" of challenged claims (section 8).
- **Skill rule:** Invalidity mode: challenge only claims you can show element by element; padding a petition with weak claims now risks denial of the whole petition.

Compact institution and review cases:
- *Thryv, Inc. v. Click-to-Call Techs., LP*, 590 U.S. 45 (2020): the Board's application of the 315(b) one-year time bar is part of the institution decision and unreviewable. The Board follows the Federal Circuit's view that service of a complaint starts the clock even if the suit is later dismissed without prejudice (*Click-to-Call Techs., LP v. Ingenio, Inc.*, 899 F.3d 1321 (Fed. Cir. 2018) (en banc in relevant part)). **Skill rule:** Invalidity mode: compute the 315(b) date from the first service on the client or any RPI or privy, regardless of later dismissal.
- *Return Mail, Inc. v. U.S. Postal Serv.*, 587 U.S. 618 (2019): a federal agency is not a "person" who can petition for IPR, PGR, or CBM review.
- *Applications in Internet Time, LLC v. RPX Corp.*, 897 F.3d 1336 (Fed. Cir. 2018): "real party in interest" is read broadly; a membership or paid challenger acting for a time-barred client inherits the bar. **Skill rule:** Invalidity mode: list every entity that funds, directs, or benefits from the petition (indemnitors, customers, members) and test each against 315(b).
- *Facebook, Inc. v. Windy City Innovations, LLC*, 973 F.3d 1321 (Fed. Cir. 2020): 315(c) joinder does not let a party join its own earlier proceeding or add new issues through joinder; the time bar exception for joinder is narrow.
- *Mylan Labs. Ltd. v. Janssen Pharmaceutica, N.V.*, 989 F.3d 1375 (Fed. Cir. 2021): no appeal from a denial of institution; mandamus only in extraordinary cases. Later: *Apple Inc. v. Vidal*, 63 F.4th 1 (Fed. Cir. 2023) (314(d) bars APA attacks on the substance of institution instructions; notice-and-comment claim allowed) and *Apple Inc. v. Squires*, No. 2024-1864 (Fed. Cir. Feb. 13, 2026) (precedential; Fintiv instructions were general policy statements needing no notice and comment); *In re Motorola Solutions, Inc.*, No. 2025-134 (Fed. Cir. Nov. 6, 2025) (precedential; mandamus cannot be used as an end run around 314(d)).
- *Qualcomm Inc. v. Apple Inc.*, 24 F.4th 1367 (Fed. Cir. 2022) and 134 F.4th 1355 (Fed. Cir. 2025) (verified): applicant-admitted prior art (statements in the challenged patent itself) is not "prior art consisting of patents or printed publications" and cannot form the basis, or part of the basis, of an IPR ground, even combined with real references; it may still show background knowledge. **Skill rule:** Invalidity charts for IPR: every ground's basis must be a patent or printed publication; mark AAPA as background only.
- *Lynk Labs, Inc. v. Samsung Elecs. Co.*, 125 F.4th 1120 (Fed. Cir. 2025), cert. denied Mar. 9, 2026 (No. 25-308) (verified): a published US patent application is IPR prior art as of its filing date (pre-AIA 102(e)), even though it published after the challenged patent's priority date. **Skill rule:** Phase 2 / invalidity: search published applications filed before the critical date, not only documents public before it.

## 4. Claim construction at the Board

### PPC Broadband, Inc. v. Corning Optical Commc'ns RF, LLC, 815 F.3d 747 (Fed. Cir. 2016) · CB p. 625
- **Facts:** The claim required a continuity member positioned to "reside around" the connector body. Under BRI, the Board picked the broadest dictionary sense, "in the immediate vicinity of; near," and found the claims obvious.
- **Holding / rule:** The broadest reasonable interpretation must be reasonable in light of the claims and specification; choosing the broadest dictionary entry is not enough. The spec used "around" seven times, always meaning encircle or surround, and used "near" separately; so "reside around" means encircle or surround (not necessarily completely). Vacated and remanded.
- **Nuance:** The "different terms, different meanings" canon carries little weight when one term is in a non-limiting preamble. A construction need not cover every disclosed embodiment; with 20+ embodiments and many claims, some claims may not read on some embodiments. (The remark that BRI versus Phillips was outcome-determinative comes from the companion appeal decided the same day, No. 2015-1361, on the term "continuity member," not from this one.)
- **Status:** BRI in AIA trials is superseded for petitions filed on or after Nov. 13, 2018 (83 FR 51340). The reasoning still governs BRI in examination, reissue, and ex parte reexamination, and it is good Phillips reasoning too.
- **Skill rule:** Office-action tool: when an examiner adopts a dictionary-broad reading, count and quote how the specification uses the term (and contrasting terms) and argue that reading is unreasonable in light of the spec (PPC Broadband). Drafting: use each technical term consistently, and use a different word when a different meaning is intended.

Construction notes (compact):
- 2018 rule (verified): 37 CFR 42.100(b), 42.200(b), 42.300(b) now use the civil-action standard and require the Board to consider prior court or ITC constructions timely made of record. **Skill rule:** Invalidity mode: put any existing Markman order in the petition record and explain alignment or departure.
- Expired patents were construed under Phillips even before 2018, because they cannot be amended (*In re Rambus Inc.*, 753 F.3d 1253 (Fed. Cir. 2014)).
- Statements by a patent owner in an IPR (including a preliminary response) can create prosecution disclaimer that narrows the claims in later litigation (*Aylus Networks, Inc. v. Apple Inc.*, 856 F.3d 1353 (Fed. Cir. 2017)). **Skill rule:** Patent-owner side: draft IPR arguments as if they were prosecution history, because they are.

## 5. Amending claims in an AIA trial

### Aqua Products, Inc. v. Matal, 872 F.3d 1290 (Fed. Cir. 2017) (en banc) · CB p. 613
- **Facts:** In an IPR on a pool-cleaner patent, Aqua moved to substitute three narrower claims. The Board agreed the amendments were non-broadening, supported, and responsive, but denied the motion because Aqua had not proven the substitute claims patentable (following its own panel decisions Idle Free and MasterImage, which also required distinguishing art known to the owner).
- **Holding / rule:** Fractured court; only two conclusions carry the judgment: the USPTO had no rule, entitled to deference, placing on the patent owner the burden of persuading on patentability of amended claims; and absent such a rule, the Board may not impose that burden. The Board must decide the patentability of entered substitute claims on the entire record. The lead opinion read 316(e) to put the burden of proving unpatentability on the petitioner for all claims; the patent owner carries only the threshold burden (one motion, reasonable number of substitutes, no broadening, no new matter, responsive to a ground).
- **Nuance:** A majority thought the statute ambiguous, so the Office could later regulate by notice-and-comment rulemaking. Concern about "untested" amended claims was answered by noting petitioners almost always contest them and the Board can reopen the record.
- **Status:** Superseded and refined by rule, as of 2026-11-18. 85 FR 82923 (Dec. 21, 2020, eff. Jan. 21, 2021): petitioner bears the burden of persuasion on unpatentability of substitute claims; patent owner bears the burden on statutory and regulatory requirements; the Board may, in the interests of justice and in rare cases, grant or deny on readily identifiable evidence of record. 89 FR 76421 (Sept. 18, 2024, eff. Oct. 18, 2024): makes the motion-to-amend pilot permanent (optional preliminary guidance from the Board and one revised motion), applies preponderance to any Board-raised ground, and lets the Board consider all evidence of record and examination assistance. The Board may raise unpatentability grounds against substitute claims sua sponte (*Nike, Inc. v. Adidas AG*, 955 F.3d 45 (Fed. Cir. 2020); see also *Hunting Titan, Inc. v. DynaEnergetics Europe GmbH*, IPR2018-00600, Paper 67 (PTAB 2020), formerly precedential, de-designated Oct. 18, 2024, when the 2024 rule took effect). Aqua's Chevron discussion is historical after *Loper Bright* (2024).
- **Skill rule:** Portfolio / patent-owner side: treat the IPR motion to amend as a narrow, one-shot tool (request preliminary guidance; expect new art); amended or new claims that survive carry intervening rights under 318(c). Keep a continuation pending when a family faces IPR risk, but remember the patent-owner estoppel in 37 CFR 42.73(d)(3): after an adverse judgment, the owner cannot obtain a claim that is not patentably distinct from a canceled or finally refused claim.

## 6. Estoppel and the effect of PTAB judgments

Statute (CB pp. 574–578): after a final written decision, the IPR petitioner, RPI, or privy may not assert in court or the ITC that a claim is invalid on any ground it "raised or reasonably could have raised" during the IPR (315(e)(2)), nor request or maintain an Office proceeding on such a ground (315(e)(1)). PGR estoppel is the same over a much wider set of grounds (325(e)).

Compact estoppel cases:
- *California Inst. of Tech. v. Broadcom Ltd.*, 25 F.4th 976 (Fed. Cir. 2022): estoppel reaches every claim and ground challenged in the final written decision and every ground that reasonably could have been included in the petition, not just instituted grounds (overruling the narrower *Shaw* reading). **Skill rule:** Invalidity mode: before filing, list every patent and printed-publication ground a skilled searcher would find; assume all of them are lost in court after a final decision.
- *Ingenico Inc. v. IOENGINE, LLC*, No. 2023-1367 (Fed. Cir. May 7, 2025) (verified): "ground" means the invalidity theory (102 or 103 based on patents or printed publications), not the documents. Estoppel does not bar court grounds based on products or public use, even if the same publications are used as evidence. **Skill rule:** Invalidity mode: preserve physical-product, public-use, and on-sale evidence for court, where IPR estoppel does not reach.
- *Kroy IP Holdings, LLC v. Groupon, Inc.*, No. 2023-1359 (Fed. Cir. Feb. 10, 2025) (verified), following *ParkerVision, Inc. v. Qualcomm Inc.*, 116 F.4th 1345 (Fed. Cir. 2024): a Board unpatentability decision on some claims does not collaterally estop the patentee from asserting other claims in court, because the Board's preponderance standard is lower than the court's clear-and-convincing standard. Contrast *XY, LLC v. Trans Ova Genetics, L.C.*, 890 F.3d 1282 (Fed. Cir. 2018): an affirmed unpatentability decision binds as to the same claims. **Skill rule:** Portfolio: unchallenged claims of a partly canceled patent remain assertable; report them separately.
- *In re Gesture Tech. Partners, LLC*, No. 2025-1075 (Fed. Cir. Dec. 1, 2025) (precedential): 315(e)(1) does not terminate an ex parte reexamination already ordered, because the requester has no further role to "maintain"; and the Office keeps jurisdiction over expired patents. **Skill rule:** Invalidity mode: an ex parte reexamination request filed early can survive a later IPR final decision; consider filing both, with different art.
- Sotera stipulations (*Sotera Wireless, Inc. v. Masimo Corp.*, IPR2020-01019, Paper 12 (PTAB 2020) (precedential as to sec. II.A)): a petitioner's stipulation not to pursue in court any ground raised or reasonably raisable in the IPR. After Ingenico the stipulation can give up more than statutory estoppel would; the proposed rule would require a broader waiver (section 8).

## 7. Stays pending PTAB review; CBM

### Versata Software, Inc. v. Callidus Software, Inc., 771 F.3d 1368 (Fed. Cir. 2014) · CB p. 642
- **Facts:** Versata sued on three sales-management patents. Callidus filed CBM petitions on 101 grounds against all claims of one patent and the independent claims of the others, then a second set covering the rest. The district court stayed the case only as to the fully challenged patent, citing Callidus's partial challenge, its own counterclaims, and its earlier motions.
- **Holding / rule:** Under the four AIA sec. 18(b)(1) factors (simplification, stage of the case, undue prejudice or tactical advantage, burden of litigation), with the more searching appellate review sec. 18(b)(2) allows, denial of the stay was error. No categorical rule that a stay is improper unless every asserted claim is under review; simplification asks what review would resolve versus what would remain; the burden factor looks forward, not at past motion practice; seeking to stay the whole case, counterclaims included, is not a "sword and shield" tactic.
- **Nuance:** Stays are weaker when sought on the eve of trial or before institution. The CBM-specific interlocutory appeal and de novo-capable review do not exist for IPR stays.
- **Status:** CBM program sunset Sept. 16, 2020 (AIA sec. 18(a)(3)); no new CBM petitions. For IPR and PGR, stays remain discretionary under the usual three-factor test, without the sec. 18 appeal right. Since 2020, the stay question interacts with discretionary denial: a court's early trial date can now cause the Office to deny institution (section 8), so the stay may never be needed.
- **Skill rule:** FTO next steps: when proposing an IPR as a litigation response, state the expected institution timing against the court's trial date and note that the Office may deny institution because of it.

CBM note cases (compact): *Versata Dev. Grp., Inc. v. SAP Am., Inc.*, 793 F.3d 1306 (Fed. Cir. 2015) (101 is available in CBM review; CBM status reviewable on appeal of a final decision); *Unwired Planet, LLC v. Google Inc.*, 841 F.3d 1376 (Fed. Cir. 2016) (a patent is not a CBM patent merely because its claims are "incidental to" financial activity). Historical only.

## 8. Discretionary denial and institution policy, 2018–2026 (verify before every use)

This area changed by memo, not statute. Re-check uspto.gov (PTAB "Interim Director Discretionary Process" page and Director decisions) and federalregister.gov before advising.

Foundations (PTAB precedential decisions): *General Plastic Indus. Co. v. Canon Kabushiki Kaisha*, IPR2016-01357, Paper 19 (2017) (serial petitions); *NHK Spring Co. v. Intri-Plex Techs., Inc.*, IPR2018-00752, Paper 8 (2018) (parallel litigation); *Apple Inc. v. Fintiv, Inc.*, IPR2020-00019, Paper 11 (2020) (six factors for parallel litigation: stay, trial date vs. final-decision date, investment, overlap, same parties, other circumstances including merits); *Advanced Bionics, LLC v. MED-EL Elektromedizinische Geräte GmbH*, IPR2019-01469, Paper 6 (2020) (325(d): same or substantially the same art or arguments already before the Office, unless the Office materially erred).

Timeline (verified unless marked):
- June 21, 2022: Director Vidal memo limited Fintiv denials (no denial for "compelling merits," ITC excluded, Sotera stipulation honored). Rescinded Feb. 28, 2025.
- Mar. 26, 2025: Acting Director Stewart's "Interim Processes for PTAB Workload Management" split institution into a Director-decided discretionary stage (separate briefs) and a merits stage; factors include the parties' "settled expectations," such as how long the claims have been in force.
- June 6, 2025: *iRhythm Techs., Inc. v. Welch Allyn, Inc.*, IPR2025-00363 et al. (Acting Director): denials on settled expectations (patent in force since 2012; petitioner knew of it by 2013, including through its own IDS), even though Fintiv factors favored institution. *Dabico Airport Sols. Inc. v. AXA Power ApS*, IPR2025-00408, Paper 21 (June 18, 2025) (designated precedential Jan. 9, 2026): no bright-line age, but longer in force means more settled; actual notice not required.
- Oct. 17, 2025: Director Squires's "Director Institution of AIA Trial Proceedings" memo: from Oct. 20, 2025 the Director decided institution of all IPRs and PGRs (discretionary and merits), usually by summary notice, consulting at least three judges. Same day: NPRM "Revision to Rules of Practice Before the PTAB," 90 FR 48335 (comment period extended to Dec. 2, 2025): would bar IPR where claims were previously upheld in any forum, or where another forum is likely to decide 102/103 validity before the final decision is due, and would require petitioners to stipulate away 102/103 prior-art defenses elsewhere, with a Director exception for extraordinary circumstances; IPR only, not PGR or reexam. The April 2024 discretionary-denial NPRM (89 FR 28693) was withdrawn (90 FR 48342).
- May 14, 2026: *Magnolia Med. Techs., Inc. v. Kurin, Inc.*, IPR2026-00097, Paper 17 (Director) (precedential): the Director's framework for discretionary considerations (cited in the Nov. 2026 memo; it denied institution where a jury had already found the patent not invalid and the petitioner sought a second bite after its invalidity expert was excluded).
- July 22, 2026: the 90 FR 48335 final rule went to OIRA for review (reginfo.gov list of rules under EO 12866 review, run date 2026-11-18: received July 22, 2026, stage "Final Rule," still under review). As of 2026-11-18 the Federal Register shows no final rule under RIN 0651-AD89. Treat the proposed bars as not in force.
- Courts: every route to review has failed. Mandamus denials include *In re Motorola Solutions* (precedential, Nov. 6, 2025) and *In re Cambridge Indus.* (nonprecedential, Dec. 9, 2025), plus *In re Google LLC* (Jan. 27, 2026), *In re Kahoot! AS* (Feb. 25, 2026), and *In re Tesla, Inc.* (Feb. 27, 2026) (all nonprecedential). *Google LLC v. VirtaMove Corp.*, cert. denied Oct. 19, 2026 (No. 25-1230). *Tesla, Inc. v. Granite Vehicle Ventures LLC* (No. 26-136) and *Intel Corp. v. Squires* (No. 26-73) were distributed for the Dec. 4, 2026 conference.
- Nov. 10, 2026: Squires/Duffy memo "Returning Merits-Based Considerations to PTAB," effective Nov. 16, 2026 (read in full). The Director still decides non-merits (discretionary) issues first, by written opinion or summary notice. Petitions that pass go to a three-judge panel that decides merits-based institution on the Director's behalf; if instituted, a different panel tries the case. Panel guidelines: (1) the petition must show element by element where the art discloses or suggests each limitation, and pointing to the examiner's findings is not enough; (2)–(4) institution is denied if the petitioner fails the standard for a "substantial majority" of challenged claims, whether shown by the patent owner or found by the panel itself; (5) a claim on which the evidence is in equipoise does not meet the reasonable-likelihood (or more-likely-than-not) standard. A prior adjudication upholding the patent or a related patent can also show the burden is not met (citing *Hulu, LLC v. Piranha Media Distrib.*, IPR2024-01252, Paper 27 (Director Apr. 17, 2025)). The memo reports discretionary denials fell from about 60% of petitions (June 2025) to 15% (Sept. 2026).
- Related: proposed rule requiring ex parte reexamination requesters to identify all real parties in interest (91 FR 46038, July 22, 2026; comments closed Aug. 21, 2026; not final as of 2026-11-18).

**Skill rule:** Invalidity mode / FTO: never present IPR as a likely path without a discretionary-denial screen: patent age and the client's prior knowledge (IDS citations, notice letters, licensing talks), parallel trial timing, prior validity rulings, art already before the examiner, and the strength of the petition across a substantial majority of the claims. Offer ex parte reexamination and PGR (if within 9 months) as alternatives, and date-stamp the policy relied on.

## 9. Derivation

- Statute: an applicant may petition within one year after the earlier of the grant of a patent, or publication of an application, claiming the same or substantially the same invention (35 U.S.C. 135(a)(2)), with a corroborated showing that the earlier filer's named inventor derived it and filed without authorization (CB p. 590; 37 CFR 42.405).
- *Global Health Solutions LLC v. Selner*, No. 2023-2009 (Fed. Cir. Aug. 26, 2025) (precedential): first Federal Circuit review of an AIA derivation proceeding. The petitioner must prove conception and communication of the invention to the respondent before the respondent's filing date; the respondent wins by proving independent conception, whoever conceived first. The Board's first-to-invent framing was error but harmless; time-stamped emails with an authenticating declaration corroborated independent conception. **Skill rule:** Phase 1 / portfolio: when a client says "they stole our idea," ask for dated, corroborated proof of what was communicated to whom and when, and docket the one-year derivation window from the other side's earliest publication or grant.

## Edge-case playbook

1. **"File an IPR on the on-sale bar, 101, or lack of enablement."** Trap: IPR covers everything. Correct: IPR is limited to 102/103 on patents and printed publications; use PGR (9 months from grant, AIA patents only) or court. 35 U.S.C. 311(b), 321(b).
2. **The petition's best combination uses the patent's own "background" admission.** Trap: AAPA is fair game with a real reference. Correct: AAPA cannot be the basis or part of the basis of a ground; use it only for background knowledge. Qualcomm (2022, 2025).
3. **Key reference is a US application filed before the priority date but published after.** Trap: not "printed" in time. Correct: it is IPR prior art as of its filing date. Lynk Labs (cert. denied 2026).
4. **The client was served with a complaint 14 months ago; the case was dismissed without prejudice.** Trap: dismissal resets the clock. Correct: the 315(b) bar still applies, and the Board's application of it is unreviewable. Click-to-Call; Thryv.
5. **A customer the client indemnifies was sued 13 months ago.** Trap: only the named defendant is barred. Correct: RPIs and privies are barred too, read broadly. Applications in Internet Time.
6. **The client filed a DJ action alleging invalidity, then wants an IPR.** Trap: harmless. Correct: 315(a)(1) bars the IPR; an invalidity counterclaim does not (315(a)(3)).
7. **A pre-AIA patent issued two months ago.** Trap: wait 9 months. Correct: the 9-month wait applies only to first-inventor-to-file patents; PGR is not available for this patent. 311(c); Pub. L. 112-274 sec. 1(d).
8. **Petition challenges 20 claims; strong on 3.** Trap: one claim suffices and SAS brings the rest in. Correct: since Nov. 16, 2026, panels deny if the showing fails for a substantial majority of challenged claims. Nov. 10, 2026 memo; SAS.
9. **Petition relies on the examiner's own findings from prosecution.** Trap: the Office already agreed. Correct: insufficient; element-by-element analysis is required, and art already before the Office invites a 325(d) denial. Nov. 10, 2026 memo; Advanced Bionics.
10. **Competitor patent in force 9 years; the client cited it in its own IDS 7 years ago.** Trap: the merits are strong, so IPR will be instituted. Correct: settled-expectations denial risk is high. iRhythm. Also, in FTO work, record when the client first learned of a patent, because it cuts both ways.
11. **Court trial is set before the final written decision would issue.** Trap: file anyway, stay will follow. Correct: Fintiv-type denial risk; the proposed rule would make it a bar (not in force as of 2026-11-18). Versata stay analysis matters only if review is instituted.
12. **IPR lost; at trial the defendant offers the same patents plus a physical product showing public use.** Trap: all estopped. Correct: the product-based public-use ground is not estopped; pure patent-and-publication grounds that reasonably could have been raised are. Ingenico; CalTech.
13. **Board canceled claims 1–5; patentee now sues on claims 6–10.** Trap: collateral estoppel. Correct: no, different burdens; only the same, affirmed claims are precluded. Kroy; ParkerVision; XY.
14. **Patent owner wants to fix claims in the IPR.** Trap: amend freely, then sue on everything. Correct: one motion, narrower substitutes only, no new matter, responsive to a ground; petitioner bears the unpatentability burden but the Board may raise new grounds; amended claims carry intervening rights; statements made can create disclaimer; after adverse judgment, 42.73(d)(3) blocks patentably indistinct claims in continuations. Aqua Products; 2020 and 2024 rules; Aylus.
15. **Claim survived an IPR, now in ex parte reexam.** Trap: same result. Correct: reexam uses BRI while the IPR used Phillips (post-2018), so the claim can fare worse. 83 FR 51340; Cuozzo.
16. **The Board instituted on a ground the petition did not clearly plead.** Trap: appeal the institution. Correct: unreviewable; fight the merits in the final decision. Cuozzo. But a final decision that skips challenged claims or grounds is reviewable. SAS.
17. **Discretionary denial looks unlawful.** Trap: appeal, APA suit, or mandamus. Correct: every route has failed through Nov. 2026 (Apple v. Squires; Motorola; VirtaMove cert. denied); two cert petitions pending for Dec. 4, 2026. Plan for court or reexam instead.
18. **The patent expired last year.** Trap: nothing to challenge. Correct: past damages keep it live; IPR and reexam remain available, construed under Phillips. Apple v. Gesture; In re Gesture.
19. **A federal agency or a government contractor sued under 28 U.S.C. 1498 wants IPR.** Trap: any defendant can petition. Correct: the agency cannot (not a "person"); check whether a private party can. Return Mail.
20. **"They stole our idea and filed first."** Trap: prove you invented first. Correct: under the AIA, prove conception and communication to them before their filing; their independent conception defeats derivation even if later; file within one year of their grant or publication. Global Health Solutions v. Selner; 35 U.S.C. 135.
21. **Client wants a CBM review of a fintech patent in 2026.** Trap: CBM is the 101 tool. Correct: the program ended Sept. 16, 2020; use PGR (if within 9 months), court, or reexam (which cannot raise 101).
22. **An ex parte reexam is pending when the IPR final decision issues against the same requester's members.** Trap: 315(e)(1) kills the reexam. Correct: an ordered reexam continues. In re Gesture.

## Checklist for the skill

- Classify the patent: AIA or pre-AIA; grant date; PGR window open?; expired?
- Compute 315(b) from the first service of a complaint on the client, any RPI, or privy; check 315(a) (prior DJ of invalidity).
- Confirm every ground's basis is a patent or printed publication (no AAPA, no products); route other grounds to PGR or court.
- Draft or review the petition element by element, claim by claim; drop claims you cannot carry so that the petition succeeds on a substantial majority.
- Run the discretionary screen: patent age and settled expectations, client's prior knowledge (IDS, letters), parallel trial dates, prior validity rulings, art already considered (325(d)), serial or parallel petitions.
- Map estoppel per ground (CalTech, Ingenico) and preserve product and public-use evidence for court.
- Compare alternatives: ex parte reexam (BRI and the SNQ standard; no Fintiv or settled-expectations briefing, but 325(d) and *In re Vivint* still apply to repeat art; RPI rule pending), PGR, district court.
- Patent-owner side: discretionary-denial brief first, then merits; treat every statement as potential disclaimer; plan any motion to amend with preliminary guidance; weigh intervening rights and 42.73(d)(3) before relying on continuations.
- After a final decision: Director Review, then appeal (Article III standing for a losing petitioner is not automatic; confirm concrete injury).
- Before advising, re-check: the Federal Register for a final rule under RIN 0651-AD89; uspto.gov for the current institution memo; the Supreme Court dockets 26-136 and 26-73.
- Never state institution or cancellation as certain; give risk factors and the date of the policy relied on.
