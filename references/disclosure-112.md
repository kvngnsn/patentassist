# Disclosure and claims: 35 U.S.C. 112

## 1. Statute map

- 112(a): written description; enablement ("make and use"); best mode.
- 112(b): claims must "particularly point out and distinctly claim" the invention (definiteness).
- 112(c)-(e): independent, dependent, and multiple dependent claim forms. A dependent claim must further limit (112(d)).
- 112(f): means- or step-plus-function elements cover the corresponding structure, material, or acts in the specification and equivalents.
- MPEP 2161: written description, enablement, and best mode are separate requirements.

## 2. Written description (112(a); MPEP 2163)

- Test: the specification must show the inventor was "in possession" of the claimed invention as of the filing date, judged by a skilled artisan reading the four corners of the document. *Ariad Pharms., Inc. v. Eli Lilly & Co.*, 598 F.3d 1336 (Fed. Cir. 2010) (en banc) (written description is separate from enablement).
- Genus claims: written description "may be satisfied through sufficient description of a representative number of species ... or by disclosure of relevant, identifying characteristics," such as structure, or function coupled with a known or disclosed structure-function correlation (MPEP 2163(II)(A)(3)(a)(ii)). When the genus varies a lot, more species are needed.
- Original claims are part of the disclosure but do not by themselves guarantee support for a broad functional genus (Ariad).
- Amended or new claims must find support in the original filing. New matter is barred (35 U.S.C. 132(a); MPEP 2163.05 changes to claim scope).
- Recent:
  - *In re Xencor, Inc.*, No. 24-1870 (Fed. Cir. Mar. 13, 2025) (precedential). "[T]he limiting preamble of a Jepson claim must be supported with sufficient written description." Sufficiency depends on what the skilled artisan knows. Affirmed the Board.
  - *Teva Pharms. Int'l GmbH v. Eli Lilly & Co.*, No. 24-1094 (Fed. Cir. Apr. 16, 2026) (precedential). Reversed JMOL that method-of-treatment claims (treating headache with humanized anti-CGRP antagonist antibodies) lacked written description and enablement. Secondary sources report the court distinguished *Amgen* because the claims were to a method of using a known antibody class, not to the antibody genus itself (exact reasoning not read: unconfirmed beyond the reversal).

## 3. Enablement (112(a); MPEP 2164)

- Test: a skilled artisan can make and use the full scope of the claimed invention without undue experimentation (MPEP 2164.01).
- *In re Wands* factors (MPEP 2164.01(a)): breadth of claims; nature of the invention; state of the prior art; level of ordinary skill; level of predictability; amount of direction provided; existence of working examples; quantity of experimentation needed.
- *Amgen Inc. v. Sanofi*, 598 U.S. 594 (2023): "If a patent claims an entire class of processes, machines, manufactures, or compositions of matter, the patent's specification must enable a person skilled in the art to make and use the entire class." Some experimentation is allowed; a "research assignment" to find working members by trial and error is not enough for a broad functional genus.
- USPTO guidelines after Amgen: 89 FR 1563 (Jan. 10, 2024). Examiners keep using the Wands factors.
- Enablement must be commensurate with claim scope (MPEP 2164.08). Prophetic examples are allowed but must be written in the present or future tense, not past tense (MPEP 2164.02).

## 4. Definiteness (112(b); MPEP 2173)

- Courts: a claim is indefinite if, read in light of the specification and prosecution history, it fails to inform skilled artisans about the scope "with reasonable certainty." *Nautilus, Inc. v. Biosig Instruments, Inc.*, 572 U.S. 898 (2014).
- USPTO during examination: "The Office does not interpret claims when examining patent applications in the same manner as the courts" (MPEP 2173.02(I), citing *In re Packard*, 751 F.3d 1307 (Fed. Cir. 2014)). Claims get BRI, and the applicant has "an opportunity and a duty to amend ambiguous claims." Expect examiners to reject unclear language that a court might tolerate.
- Watch list (MPEP 2173.05): relative terms ("about," "substantially") need a standard in the spec (2173.05(b)); lack of antecedent basis (2173.05(e)); functional limitations need clear boundaries (2173.05(g)); alternatives and Markush groups (2173.05(h)); negative limitations need support (2173.05(i)); trademarks in claims (2173.05(u)); omnibus claims (2173.05(r)).
- Breadth alone is not indefiniteness (MPEP 2173.04).
- Example: *GoTV Streaming v. Netflix* (Fed. Cir. Feb. 9, 2026) reversed a district court's indefiniteness ruling on "discrete low level rendering command" and adopted the patentee's construction from intrinsic evidence (the claims then failed under 101).

## 5. Means-plus-function and nonce words (112(f); MPEP 2181-2186)

- *Williamson v. Citrix Online, LLC*, 792 F.3d 1339 (Fed. Cir. 2015) (en banc in relevant part): no strong presumption against 112(f) when "means" is absent. Test: "whether the words of the claim are understood by persons of ordinary skill in the art to have a sufficiently definite meaning as the name for structure."
- USPTO three-prong test (MPEP 2181(I)):
  - (A) the limitation uses "means," "step," or a generic placeholder (nonce term) with no specific structural meaning;
  - (B) it is modified by functional language, often linked by "for," "configured to," or "so that";
  - (C) it is not modified by enough structure, material, or acts to perform the function.
- Common nonce terms: module, mechanism, element, unit, component, device, handler, system, engine (context controls).
- If 112(f) applies, the claim covers only the disclosed structure and equivalents. If the specification discloses no corresponding structure, the claim is indefinite under 112(b).

### Software: disclose the algorithm (MPEP 2181(II)(B))

- For a computer-implemented 112(f) limitation, "the specification must disclose an algorithm for performing the claimed specific computer function, or else the claim is indefinite." A general-purpose computer alone is not enough structure. (*Aristocrat*, *WMS Gaming*, *Net MoneyIN*, *Rain Computing*, cited there.)
- The algorithm can be a formula, prose, flowchart, or step list, with enough detail to show how the function is done.
- *Fintiv, Inc. v. PayPal Holdings, Inc.*, No. 23-2312 (Fed. Cir. Apr. 30, 2025) (precedential). "Payment handler" terms recited function without sufficient structure, so 112(f) applied (the court compared "handler" to the nonce word "module"). The specifications disclosed no algorithm; the proposed algorithm merely restated claim language. Claims indefinite.

Drafting rules for software claims:
- Prefer structural recitations: "a processor configured to execute instructions stored in memory to: [step 1], [step 2]..." and recite the steps concretely.
- Do not rely on black-box nouns ("analysis module," "payment handler") to carry the invention.
- For every functional box in a figure, describe the steps (flowchart plus prose), inputs, outputs, and data structures.
- If you choose 112(f) on purpose, map each function to a specific algorithm in the spec and say so.
- 89 FR 19817 (Mar. 20, 2024) lists USPTO resources for examining MPF and step-plus-function limitations.

## 6. Provisional and parent support for later claims

- A later claim gets the earlier date only if the earlier application supports it under 112(a) (written description and enablement), "except for the best mode requirement" (35 U.S.C. 119(e)(1), 120; MPEP 211.05).
- *New Railhead Mfg., L.L.C. v. Vermeer Mfg. Co.*, 298 F.3d 1290 (Fed. Cir. 2002): the provisional must contain a written description of the invention claimed in the nonprovisional, in terms that let a skilled artisan practice it. A claim not supported is not entitled to the provisional date, and the inventor's own intervening disclosures can then be prior art.
- *Dynamic Drinkware, LLC v. National Graphics, Inc.*, 800 F.3d 1375 (Fed. Cir. 2015): a party relying on a reference patent's provisional date must show the provisional supports the reference's claims (at least one claim).
- *In re Riggs*, No. 22-1945 (Fed. Cir. Mar. 24, 2025): additionally, the provisional must support the specific portions of the reference relied on in the rejection (pre-AIA 102(e)).
- Practical: a thin provisional (slide deck, abstract) gives little protection. Treat the provisional like a full application: claims-ready detail, alternatives, ranges, figures.
- Continuation-in-part: new matter gets the CIP's filing date; claims mixing old and new matter take the later date.

## 7. Incorporation by reference (37 CFR 1.57; MPEP 608.01(p), 2163.07(b))

- Must be in the specification, use the root words "incorporat(e)" and "reference," and clearly identify the document (1.57(c)).
- "Essential material" (needed for 112(a), 112(b), or 112(f) structure) may be incorporated only from a U.S. patent or U.S. patent application publication that does not itself incorporate it by reference (1.57(d)).
- Nonessential material may come from U.S. patents and publications, foreign patents and published applications, commonly owned U.S. applications, and non-patent publications (1.57(e)).
- Safeguard: if part of the specification or drawings is inadvertently omitted, a priority or benefit claim present on the filing date is treated as an incorporation by reference of the omitted part if it is completely contained in the prior application (1.57(b)).
- Defective incorporations must be fixed before close of prosecution or abandonment (1.57(h)). Inserting incorporated text later must be by amendment with a no-new-matter statement (1.57(g)).
- Drafting rule: copy essential material into the specification. Do not incorporate essential content from websites, standards, or papers.

## 8. Best mode (MPEP 2165)

- Still a requirement of 112(a): the specification must "set forth the best mode contemplated by the inventor or joint inventor."
- AIA sec. 15 amended 35 U.S.C. 282 (effective Sept. 16, 2011): failure to disclose best mode "shall not be a basis on which any claim of a patent may be canceled or held invalid or otherwise unenforceable." This does not change examination practice (MPEP 2165(II)).
- Examiners rarely reject for best mode; they need evidence of concealment (MPEP 2165.03-2165.04).
- Drafting rule: still disclose the preferred embodiment. Concealment can create inequitable-conduct and foreign-filing risk (foreign risk: general practice, unconfirmed for any specific country).

## 9. Spec-drafting checklist (112)

- [ ] Every claim term has literal or clear support; define key terms.
- [ ] Each genus has several species and the shared structure or mechanism.
- [ ] Ranges: broad, intermediate, narrow, with endpoints.
- [ ] Working examples where possible; prophetic examples in present or future tense.
- [ ] Software: algorithm for every claimed function (steps, flowchart, data structures).
- [ ] No black-box nonce terms carrying the novelty; or, if 112(f) intended, explicit structure mapping.
- [ ] Relative terms have an objective standard.
- [ ] Antecedent basis checked in every claim.
- [ ] Essential material copied in, not incorporated from non-patent sources.
- [ ] Preferred embodiment disclosed.
- [ ] Provisional contains enough detail to support the claims you will later file.
