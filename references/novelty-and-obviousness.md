# Novelty and obviousness

Applies to applications with an effective filing date on or after March 16, 2013 (AIA first-inventor-to-file). Older applications use pre-AIA 102 (MPEP 2131-2138). Statute pin cites are to current 35 U.S.C.

## 1. Effective filing date (35 U.S.C. 100(i))

- Per claim, not per application.
- It is the actual filing date, or the earliest priority/benefit date (119, 120, 121, 365, 386) for an application that *supports that claim* under 112(a).
- A claim that adds matter not in the provisional or parent gets the later date. See disclosure-112.md (New Railhead).

## 2. What is prior art: 102(a)

- 102(a)(1): the claimed invention was "patented, described in a printed publication, or in public use, on sale, or otherwise available to the public" before the effective filing date. Worldwide. Any language.
- 102(a)(2): a U.S. patent, a published U.S. application, or a WIPO-published PCT designating the U.S. (deemed published under 122(b)) that "names another inventor" and was "effectively filed" before the claim's effective filing date. Secret prior art: it counts as of its effective filing date even though published later.
- 102(d): a 102(a)(2) reference is effectively filed as of its earliest priority/benefit application "that describes the subject matter." See *In re Riggs* below.

### Printed publication (MPEP 2128)

- Test: public accessibility. A skilled person exercising reasonable diligence could locate it (MPEP 2128.01).
- Online posts, theses indexed in a library, conference posters shown without restriction can count. Confidential documents do not.
- Date = when it became accessible (MPEP 2128.02).

### Public use (MPEP 2152.02(c))

- Use by the inventor or others, not under confidentiality, before the effective filing date.
- Experimental use can negate public use in some cases (pre-AIA case law; MPEP 2133.03(e)).

### On sale (MPEP 2152.02(d))

- *Pfaff v. Wells Electronics*, 525 U.S. 55 (1998): (1) a commercial offer for sale or sale, and (2) the invention was ready for patenting.
- *Helsinn Healthcare S.A. v. Teva Pharms. USA, Inc.*, 139 S. Ct. 628 (2019): Congress did not change the meaning of "on sale" in the AIA; "an inventor's sale of an invention to a third party who is obligated to keep the invention confidential can qualify as prior art under § 102(a)."
- No geographic limit on sales under AIA 102(a)(1) (MPEP 2152.02(d)).
- Practical rule: confidential supply or manufacturing deals can start the clock.

### Otherwise available to the public (MPEP 2152.02(e))

- Catch-all for public disclosures not fitting the other categories (e.g., public talk).

## 3. Exceptions: 102(b)

### 102(b)(1): grace period for 102(a)(1) art (MPEP 2153)

A disclosure made 1 year or less before the effective filing date is not prior art if:
- (A) it was made by the inventor, a joint inventor, or another who obtained it from them (MPEP 2153.01(a)); or
- (B) the same subject matter was publicly disclosed earlier by the inventor or someone who got it from the inventor (MPEP 2153.02). This shield covers only the same subject matter, read narrowly. Variations in the intervening third-party disclosure may still be prior art.

### 102(b)(2): exceptions for 102(a)(2) art (MPEP 2154)

A U.S. patent or application disclosure is not prior art if:
- (A) its subject matter was obtained from the inventor;
- (B) the inventor publicly disclosed it before the reference was effectively filed; or
- (C) the reference and the claimed invention were commonly owned, or subject to an obligation of assignment to the same person, not later than the claimed invention's effective filing date. 102(c) treats parties to a joint research agreement as common owners if conditions are met and the application names the JRA parties.

### How to invoke

- 37 CFR 1.130(a): declaration of attribution (the disclosure is the inventor's own or obtained from the inventor).
- 37 CFR 1.130(b): declaration of prior public disclosure by the inventor.
- 102(b)(2)(C): a clear statement of common ownership or a joint research agreement (MPEP 2154.02(c), 717.02-717.02(c)).
- Declaration practice under 1.130: MPEP 717.01-717.01(f).

### Grace period warnings

- The U.S. grace period is 1 year. Most other countries have absolute novelty. A public disclosure before filing can destroy foreign rights.
- File a provisional before any public disclosure, sale offer, or pitch without NDA.

## 4. Anticipation (102) and how to chart it

- A claim is anticipated only if a single reference discloses every element, arranged as in the claim, expressly or inherently (MPEP 2131). The reference must be enabling (MPEP 2121, 2131 - presumption that a patent reference is enabled).
- Multiple-reference 102 rejections are allowed only for narrow purposes: to show the primary reference is enabled, to explain a term, or to show an inherent characteristic (MPEP 2131.01).
- Genus-species: a species anticipates a genus; a genus usually does not anticipate a species (MPEP 2131.02).
- Ranges: a prior art value inside the claimed range anticipates; overlapping ranges are usually a 103 issue unless disclosed with "sufficient specificity" (MPEP 2131.03).

### Inherent anticipation (MPEP 2112)

- The missing feature must necessarily be present in the reference, not merely probably or possibly.
- Recognition by a skilled artisan at the time is not required.
- Once the examiner gives a sound basis for inherency, the burden shifts to the applicant to show the prior art does not necessarily have the feature (MPEP 2112(IV)-(V)).

### 102 chart template

| Claim element (verbatim, numbered 1[a], 1[b]...) | Reference location (page/col:line, figure, paragraph) | Quote or description | Express / inherent | Notes (claim construction, gaps) |
|---|---|---|---|---|

Rules for the chart:
- One reference only. Every element must have a cite.
- Check the reference date against the claim's effective filing date and check 102(b) exceptions.
- Check that the elements are arranged as claimed in one embodiment, not picked from separate embodiments.
- If any row is empty, it is not anticipation. Move to 103.

## 5. Obviousness (103)

Statute: a patent may not be obtained if the differences between the claimed invention and the prior art are such that the claimed invention "as a whole would have been obvious before the effective filing date ... to a person having ordinary skill in the art." "Patentability shall not be negated by the manner in which the invention was made."

### Graham factors (*Graham v. John Deere Co.*, 383 U.S. 1 (1966); MPEP 2141)

1. Scope and content of the prior art (MPEP 2141.01). Must be analogous art: same field of endeavor, or reasonably pertinent to the inventor's problem (MPEP 2141.01(a)).
2. Differences between the prior art and the claims (MPEP 2141.02). Consider the claim as a whole.
3. Level of ordinary skill (MPEP 2141.03).
4. Objective indicia (secondary considerations) when present.

### KSR (*KSR Int'l Co. v. Teleflex Inc.*, 550 U.S. 398 (2007))

- Flexible, not rigid teaching-suggestion-motivation. Common sense and market forces count. But the examiner must give an articulated reason with rational underpinning.
- MPEP 2143, Section I rationales (verbatim headings):
  - (A) Combining prior art elements according to known methods to yield predictable results;
  - (B) Simple substitution of one known element for another to obtain predictable results;
  - (C) Use of known technique to improve similar devices (methods, or products) in the same way;
  - (D) Applying a known technique to a known device (method, or product) ready for improvement to yield predictable results;
  - (E) "Obvious to try" - choosing from a finite number of identified, predictable solutions, with a reasonable expectation of success;
  - (F) Known work in one field of endeavor may prompt variations of it for use in either the same field or a different one based on design incentives or other market forces if the variations are predictable to one of ordinary skill in the art;
  - (G) Some teaching, suggestion, or motivation in the prior art that would have led one of ordinary skill to modify the prior art reference or to combine prior art reference teachings to arrive at the claimed invention.
- The list is not exhaustive. Any rationale must link the facts to the conclusion.
- Related: 2143.01 (motivation to modify; proposed change cannot make the reference unsatisfactory for its intended purpose or change its principle of operation), 2143.02 (reasonable expectation of success required), 2143.03 (all claim limitations must be considered).
- Feb. 27, 2024 USPTO guidance, "Updated Guidance for Making a Proper Determination of Obviousness," 89 FR 14449: restates KSR flexibility and that examiners must give reasoned explanations.

### Teaching away (MPEP 2141.02(VI), 2145(X)(D))

- A reference teaches away if it criticizes, discredits, or otherwise discourages the claimed solution.
- A mere preference for an alternative is not teaching away.
- Consider the reference as a whole, including parts that point away.

### Secondary considerations (objective indicia)

- Commercial success, long-felt but unsolved need, failure of others, unexpected results, copying, industry praise, licensing, skepticism (MPEP 716.01-716.06; 2145).
- Must have a nexus to the claimed features.
- Submit by 37 CFR 1.132 declaration with facts, data, and comparison to the closest prior art (MPEP 716.02(e)).
- Common rebuttal arguments and how examiners weigh them: MPEP 2145 I-X (hindsight 2145(X)(A), obvious to try 2145(X)(B), no reason to combine 2145(X)(C), teaching away 2145(X)(D), nonanalogous art 2145(IX)).
- Unexpected results must be commensurate in scope with the claims (MPEP 716.02(d)).

### 103 combination chart template

| Claim element | Primary ref (cite) | Secondary ref (cite) | Gap filled by secondary? | Motivation to combine (with cite / rationale A-G) | Reasonable expectation of success | Counter-evidence (teaching away, change of principle, unexpected results) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|

Rules for the chart:
- Pick the closest single reference as primary. Usually no more than 2-3 references.
- For each gap, cite the secondary reference and an articulated reason to combine. Name the KSR rationale.
- Record why a skilled person would expect success.
- Check analogous-art status of each reference.
- Record counterarguments for the response: teaching away, inoperability, hindsight, missing elements, secondary considerations.

## 6. Recent developments (2024-2026)

Design patents:
- *LKQ Corp. v. GM Global Technology Operations LLC*, 102 F.4th 1280 (Fed. Cir. May 21, 2024) (en banc). Overruled the *Rosen-Durling* requirements that the primary reference be "basically the same" and that secondary references be "so related." Design obviousness now follows a Graham/KSR-style approach. USPTO issued design examination guidance May 22, 2024.
- Mar. 13, 2026: USPTO "Supplemental Guidance for Examination of Design Patent Applications Related to Computer-Generated Interfaces and Icons," 91 FR 12394 (title confirmed; content not read).

Utility (precedential Federal Circuit):
- *Honeywell Int'l Inc. v. 3G Licensing, S.A.*, 124 F.4th 1345 (Fed. Cir. Jan. 2, 2025). Reversed a PTAB nonobviousness ruling. Quoting KSR, "any need or problem known in the field of endeavor at the time of invention and addressed by the patent can provide a reason for combining"; the motivation need not be the patentee's own motivation.
- *Lynk Labs, Inc. v. Samsung Electronics Co.*, No. 23-2346 (Fed. Cir. Jan. 14, 2025). Under pre-AIA 102(e)(1), a published patent application can be IPR prior art as of its filing date, not its publication date.
- *In re Riggs*, No. 22-1945 (Fed. Cir. Mar. 24, 2025). For a reference to get its provisional's date, (1) at least one claim must be supported by the provisional (*Dynamic Drinkware*) and (2) the provisional must also support "the specific portions ... identified and relied on in the prior art rejection." Decided under pre-AIA 102(e); application to AIA 102(a)(2)/(d) not decided (unconfirmed). Practical: when a 102(a)(2) reference claims a provisional, check whether the provisional actually contains the passages the examiner cites.
- *Qualcomm Inc. v. Apple Inc.*, Nos. 23-1208, -1209 (Fed. Cir. Apr. 23, 2025). Applicant admitted prior art cannot form "the basis" of an IPR ground under 35 U.S.C. 311(b); Board's contrary reading reversed. Drafting lesson: avoid admissions in the background anyway; they can still be used as evidence of general knowledge.
- Nonprecedential and other 2025-2026 obviousness decisions reported by secondary sources (Guardant v. Univ. of Washington, Virtek Vision, Nielsen v. TVision) were not verified (unconfirmed).

USPTO:
- Automated Search Pilot (ASAP!) gives some applicants a pre-examination prior art notice. See prosecution.md.

## 7. Search-to-opinion workflow (for the skill)

1. Fix each claim's effective filing date.
2. Screen each reference: date, 102(a)(1) vs (a)(2) category, 102(b) exception, provisional support (Riggs).
3. Try a 102 chart per strong reference. Any empty row ends 102.
4. Build 103 charts from the closest reference plus one or two others, with a named rationale and expectation of success.
5. List rebuttal points and any objective evidence.
6. Never fabricate a citation. Quote locations exactly.
