# Practitioner rules and limits for a patent software product

This file is not legal advice. It lists the rules a patent-drafting tool must respect.

## 1. Who may represent others before the USPTO in patent matters

- Statute: the USPTO "may govern the recognition and conduct of agents, attorneys, or other persons representing applicants" and may require good moral character and qualifications (35 U.S.C. 2(b)(2)(D)).
- 37 CFR 11.10(a): "Only practitioners registered under § 11.6; individuals given limited recognition under § 11.9(a) or (b) or § 11.16; or individuals admitted pro hac vice as provided in § 41.5(a) or 42.10(c) ... are permitted to represent others before the Office in patent matters."
- Registration (11.6, 11.7): only individuals. 11.6(a) patent attorneys; 11.6(b) patent agents (non-attorneys); 11.6(d) design patent practitioners. Each must show good moral character, legal/scientific/technical qualifications, and competence (11.7(a)(2)), usually by passing the registration exam.
- Limited recognition (11.9): case-specific or for those ineligible due to immigration status. Law students in certified clinics (11.16).
- 11.14 covers trademark and other non-patent matters (attorneys only; non-lawyers generally not recognized). Patent registration alone does not allow trademark practice.

### What counts as "practice before the Office" (11.5(b))

- Includes "preparing necessary documents in contemplation of filing the documents with the Office," corresponding with the Office, representing a client, and "advising a client concerning matters pending or contemplated to be presented before the Office."
- Patent practice (11.5(b)(1)) includes "preparing or prosecuting any patent application; consulting with or giving advice to a client in contemplation of filing a patent application ...; drafting the specification or claims of a patent application; drafting an amendment or reply to a communication from the Office that may require written argument to establish the patentability of a claimed invention."
- Practitioners may use non-practitioner assistants under their supervision (11.5(b)).
- Federal registration lets patent agents do this work despite state unauthorized-practice laws (*Sperry v. Florida ex rel. Florida Bar*, 373 U.S. 379 (1963)). Advice outside USPTO practice (licensing, infringement, contracts) is governed by state law. 11.505 bars practitioners from practicing law in violation of a jurisdiction's rules or assisting others to do so.

## 2. Pro se rights and their limits

- An applicant or patent owner may file and prosecute their own case (37 CFR 1.31(a)), except:
  - (1) a juristic entity (e.g., a company assignee) must be represented by a patent practitioner;
  - (2) since July 20, 2026, an applicant where at least one party named as applicant is domiciled outside the U.S. or its territories;
  - (3) since July 20, 2026, a patent owner where at least one owner is domiciled outside the U.S. or its territories.
- Source: final rule 91 FR 13510 (Mar. 20, 2026), effective July 20, 2026. "Domicile" = permanent legal residence of a natural person or principal place of business of a juristic entity (1.9(p)). The rule does not change how to get a filing date. Papers for these parties must be signed by a practitioner (1.33(b)).
- Pro se papers are signed by the applicant (1.33(b)(3)). The signer must be a natural person and personally insert the signature (1.4(d); April 2024 AI guidance).
- "The Office cannot aid in the selection of a patent practitioner" (1.31(b)).
- A pro se inventor may use software to help draft their own application. That is the inventor acting for themselves. The risk rises when a business provides drafting or prosecution as a service *for others* without a registered practitioner (11.10(a), 11.5(b)(1)). The USPTO has not issued guidance on AI drafting products specifically (none found in this check).

## 3. Signatures, certifications, candor, confidentiality

- 37 CFR 11.18(a): practitioner correspondence must bear a signature "personally signed or inserted by such practitioner."
- 11.18(b): anyone presenting a paper (practitioner or not) certifies that statements are true or believed true, subject to 18 U.S.C. 1001, and that after "an inquiry reasonable under the circumstances" the paper has no improper purpose, legal contentions are warranted, and factual contentions have evidentiary support. Sanctions: 11.18(c).
- 37 CFR 1.56(a): each individual associated with filing and prosecution has "a duty of candor and good faith," including a duty to disclose information known to be material to patentability, for each pending claim. No patent will be granted where fraud was practiced or the duty was violated through bad faith or intentional misconduct. Applies to inventors, practitioners, and others substantively involved (1.56(c)).
- 37 CFR 11.106: practitioners must not reveal client information without informed consent or another exception. Entering client invention details into third-party AI may implicate this (April 2024 AI guidance).
- 37 CFR 11.804: misconduct includes dishonesty, deceit, misrepresentation (c) and conduct prejudicial to the administration of justice (d). Applied to unverified generative-AI citations in *In re Mitchell*, D2026-16 (public reprimand, 2026).

## 4. May a software product be marketed as a "patent agent" or "AI patent agent"?

Short answer: No. Do not use "patent agent," "AI patent agent," "patent attorney," "registered," or similar titles for software or for an unregistered company.

Reasons:
1. 35 U.S.C. 33 (verbatim): "Whoever, not being recognized to practice before the Patent and Trademark Office, holds himself out or permits himself to be held out as so recognized, or as being qualified to prepare or prosecute applications for patent, shall be fined not more than $1,000 for each offense." "Whoever" includes corporations and companies (1 U.S.C. 1). The second clause reaches claims of being "qualified to prepare or prosecute applications for patent," not only claims of registration. Marketing a product as an "agent" that prepares and prosecutes applications fits the conduct described. (No reported prosecution of a software company under section 33 was found; risk assessment, not a holding.)
2. "Patent Agent" is a designation for registered practitioners. 37 CFR 11.704(b): a registered practitioner under 11.6(b) who is not an attorney "may use the designation 'Patents,' 'Patent Agent,' 'Registered Patent Agent,' or a substantially similar designation." Only individuals can register (11.6, 11.7). Software cannot be registered, hold a USPTO.gov account, or sign papers (April 2024 AI guidance). Individuals with limited recognition "may not hold themselves out as being registered" (11.704(e)).
3. Representing others is limited to recognized practitioners (11.10(a)), and drafting specifications, claims, and replies for others is practice (11.5(b)(1)).
4. If the company involves registered practitioners, they are bound by 11.701 (no false or misleading communication about services; a statement is misleading if it omits a fact needed to make it not materially misleading), 11.702(d) (communications must name at least one responsible practitioner or firm), and 11.804(a) (cannot violate the rules "through the acts of another"). 35 U.S.C. 32 allows exclusion of anyone who, by advertising "with intent to defraud," deceives or misleads applicants.
5. General consumer law: the FTC's final order against DoNotPay (announced Feb. 11, 2025) bars it from advertising that its service "performs like a real lawyer" without sufficient evidence, plus $193,000 in relief. "AI patent agent" invites the same kind of claim (FTC Act section 5; analogy, not a patent-specific rule).

USPTO statements found: the USPTO's practitioner pages say only registered attorneys and agents (and those with limited recognition) may represent patent applicants, and tell the public to verify registration with its practitioner search. No USPTO statement specific to AI products was found.

### Safe wording

Use:
- "AI patent drafting assistant" / "patent drafting software"
- "Helps inventors and registered practitioners prepare draft patent documents"
- "Prior-art search and draft-preparation tool"
- "Drafts for review by a registered patent attorney or agent"
- "For pro se inventors filing their own applications, and for practitioners"

Required disclaimers (put near the top and at checkout):
- "[Product] is software. It is not a law firm, not a patent attorney, and not a registered patent agent. It is not recognized to practice before the USPTO and does not represent you."
- "Using [Product] does not create an attorney-client or agent-client relationship. Communications may not be privileged or confidential."
- "Have a registered patent practitioner review any filing. Find one at the USPTO practitioner search."
- "Companies and applicants domiciled outside the U.S. must be represented by a registered patent practitioner (37 CFR 1.31)."

Avoid:
- "patent agent," "AI patent agent," "robot patent attorney," "virtual patent lawyer"
- "registered," "USPTO-approved," "USPTO-certified," "recognized by the USPTO"
- "we file and prosecute your patent," "we respond to the examiner for you"
- "as good as a patent attorney," "guaranteed patent," "patent pending in minutes" (unless literally accurate)

## 5. Free and low-cost help (contacts verified Nov. 18, 2026)

- USPTO Pro Se Assistance Center: 866-767-3848; ProSeAssistanceCenter@uspto.gov; Mon-Fri 8:30 a.m.-5 p.m. ET; one-on-one appointments by video or phone. Staff "cannot give legal advice." Page: https://www.uspto.gov/patents/patents-ombuds/pro-se-assistance-center
- USPTO Patent Pro Bono Program: https://www.uspto.gov/patentprobono ; probono@uspto.gov. Typical eligibility: gross household income less than three times the federal poverty guidelines (regional programs vary); a provisional on file or completion of the USPTO certificate training course; ability to describe the invention. Apply through the regional program for your state.
- USPTO Law School Clinic Certification Program: https://www.uspto.gov/lawschoolclinic ; over 70 participating law school clinics; contact a clinic directly. Program contact (OED): 571-272-4097; LawSchoolInformation@uspto.gov (the page's mailto link points to lawschoolpilotinformation@uspto.gov; confirm before use).
- Verify any practitioner: USPTO "Finding a patent practitioner" page and practitioner search (https://www.uspto.gov/learning-and-resources/patent-and-trademark-practitioners/finding-patent-practitioner).

## 6. Rules for this skill

- Present itself as a drafting assistant, never as an agent or attorney.
- Tell users the output is a draft that needs review; recommend a registered practitioner and list the free resources above.
- Ask about domicile and entity type at intake. If the applicant is a company or foreign-domiciled, say a registered practitioner is required.
- Never sign, file, or log in to Patent Center for the user.
- Flag confidentiality and export-control risks before the user shares technical details.
